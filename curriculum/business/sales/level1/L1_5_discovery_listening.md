# Discovery Questions and Listening

**Level:** 1. **Scope:** Additive instructional draft with hypothetical cases; not a certified completed lesson.

## Outcomes
Ask neutral questions, distinguish observation from inference, summarize a buyer's priorities and document uncertainty without leading the conversation.

## Instruction
Discovery is an evidence-gathering conversation, not an interrogation. Open questions invite descriptions: “What happens when an inquiry arrives?” Closed questions verify specifics: “Does every request require supervisor approval?” Use both intentionally. A question should not embed an unverified claim such as “How much money does your broken system waste?”

**Listening sequence:** Ask; allow an uninterrupted answer; reflect the meaning; clarify a missing detail; summarize; request confirmation. Avoid treating silence as agreement. If a buyer mentions a sensitive issue, do not pressure them to reveal customer records or proprietary data.

**Worked dialogue:** Buyer: “Our reports take too long.” Seller: “Which report, and what steps take the most time?” Buyer: “Combining exports.” Seller: “How frequently is that done?” Buyer: “Every Friday.” Seller: “Do you have a recent estimate of the time involved?” Buyer: “About two hours.” Seller: “So the current estimate is two hours weekly for combining exports, but we haven't measured it. Is that accurate?” This preserves the distinction between a self-report and a verified baseline.

**Quantification:** A buyer estimates 2 hours weekly at an illustrative $35 hourly labor cost. Gross time value is 2 × 35 = $70 weekly, before error correction, implementation and software costs. Do not imply the buyer would realize cash savings equal to that estimate; time freed may be redeployed rather than removed from payroll.

## Exercises and solutions
1. Rewrite “Don't you agree automation is necessary?” **Answer:** “What alternatives have you considered for this workflow?”
2. Identify the unsupported inference in “You have a backlog, so you need our AI.” **Answer:** Backlog alone does not establish that this specific tool is suitable.
3. A task takes 90 minutes twice weekly. **Answer:** 180 minutes or three hours weekly.
4. Give a reflective summary that acknowledges uncertainty. **Answer:** “You estimate three hours weekly, pending measurement, and accuracy is your primary concern.”

## Mastery
Conduct a five-question roleplay with two open questions, one clarification, one reflective summary and an explicit check of buyer agreement. Transfer: interview a hypothetical school administrator about scheduling rather than sales reporting.
