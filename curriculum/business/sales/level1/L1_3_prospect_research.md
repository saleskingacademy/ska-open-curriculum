# Prospect Research and Relevance

**Level:** 1. **Type:** Additive course expansion; hypothetical cases, not verified SKA operational results.

## Instruction, worked examples and assessment

Prospect research seeks evidence that an offer may be relevant to a particular organization. It does not authorize contacting that organization or collecting personal data indiscriminately. Start with a business hypothesis, identify public or properly licensed sources, and separate observed facts from inferred needs.

**Evidence ladder:** A company website may confirm its publicly advertised service; a job posting may suggest an operational priority; a press release may describe a plan; none establishes budget, purchase authority or consent. An inference such as “this company probably needs automation” should be labeled as a hypothesis until validated.

**Research worksheet:** Record organization, source URL, access date, directly supported claim, confidence limitation, proposed relevance, contact-permission basis and next validation question. Do not copy private profiles, scrape restricted services or infer sensitive personal characteristics. Platform terms and applicable marketing rules still apply when a task is technically automatable.

**Worked example:** A fictional repair company advertises appointments through a web form and publishes weekday service hours. A seller hypothesizes that automated triage could help route inquiries outside business hours. This is not evidence that messages are missed or that the company wants automation. A responsible discovery question is “How are inquiries received outside operating hours handled today?” rather than “You are losing customers every night.”

**Prioritization:** Consider evidence strength, plausible fit, implementation complexity and downside risk. Suppose three prospects receive research scores of 4/5, 2/5 and 5/5, but the third requires an integration the seller cannot support. A high score cannot override a hard technical incompatibility. Scoring is a triage aid, not a claim of purchase probability.

**Exercise 1:** A firm announces expansion into two cities. What is observed and what remains unknown? **Solution:** Expansion announcement is observed; budget, staffing constraints and appetite for a particular tool remain unknown.

**Exercise 2:** Rewrite “Your outdated system is costing you thousands” as a neutral opening question. **Solution:** “What tools do you currently use, and where, if anywhere, do delays occur?”

**Exercise 3:** Name two conditions to verify before outreach. **Solution:** Applicable consent/legal basis and channel/platform rules; also suppression and opt-out status.

**Mastery gate:** Produce a research record with two sourced observations, two labeled hypotheses, one neutral question and an explicit contact-permission check. **Transfer:** Research a fictional school district's published procurement needs without collecting student information.
