# Sales — Negotiation and Mutual Value
Prerequisites: discovery, contribution margin, evidence-based qualification.

## Objectives
Explain BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement), reservation point, target and zone of possible agreement; distinguish positions from interests; calculate deal economics; develop ethical concessions; document agreement without unauthorized commitments.

## Lesson
Negotiation is joint decision-making under partly competing interests. A **position** is a requested outcome, such as “reduce the price by 20%.” An **interest** is the underlying concern, such as uncertain implementation risk or a budget approval ceiling. Asking why a condition matters can uncover solutions beyond price discounts.

Before negotiating, define the BATNA: the best feasible alternative if no agreement is reached. The **reservation point** is the worst acceptable outcome given the BATNA and relevant constraints. A target is the desired outcome; it is not the reservation point. The **zone of possible agreement** exists when parties' acceptable terms overlap. For multi-issue negotiations, price alone may be insufficient: scope, service levels, payment timing, delivery schedule, and risk allocation matter.

### Worked example
A service is offered for $2,499/month. Direct monthly delivery costs are estimated at $900, yielding $1,599 monthly contribution before fixed costs. A buyer requests 20% off. Discounted price is $2,499×0.80=$1,999.20, yielding $1,099.20 contribution, a $499.80 reduction per month. Over 12 months, contribution reduction is $5,997.60, assuming stable costs and retention. Do not call this reduction “profit lost” without considering alternative outcomes, customer lifetime, acquisition costs and fixed costs.

A concession should be conditional and reciprocal rather than arbitrary: “If we narrow the implementation scope and retain the service-level requirements, we can assess whether the revised price covers delivery.” No claim that the buyer must accept. If the proposed terms violate delivery capability or legal obligations, decline or redesign the offer.

## Worked dialogue
Buyer: “Your price is too high.”
Seller: “Which part of the economic case is not yet justified—expected results, implementation risk, or the budget limit?”
Buyer: “Our team worries the integration will take too long.”
Seller: “Would a scoped acceptance test with explicit security and success criteria help assess that risk before either party commits?”
This exchange tests the underlying interest without inventing a deadline.

## Quiz with answer explanations
Q1. What is a BATNA? **A:** Best feasible alternative if no agreement is reached; it anchors the decision to accept or reject terms.
Q2. What is the reservation point? **A:** The least acceptable package of terms; it is not automatically the opening offer.
Q3. What is a buyer's position? **A:** Their stated requested outcome, which may differ from the underlying interest.
Q4. Discounted monthly price at 20% off $2,499? **A:** $1,999.20 because $2,499×.80=$1,999.20.
Q5. Contribution at that price with $900 direct cost? **A:** $1,099.20.
Q6. Monthly contribution reduction versus list price? **A:** $499.80.
Q7. Is every buyer objection evidence of a price problem? **A:** No; the underlying issue may be implementation risk or missing proof of value.
Q8. Can an agent promise a concession beyond approved authority? **A:** No; it must observe contractual and authorization limits.

## Level examination
A buyer proposes $2,099/month for 18 months. Delivery cost is $975/month. List price remains $2,499/month.
A. Monthly contribution at proposed price? **Solution:** $2,099−$975=$1,124.
B. Monthly contribution at list price with the same costs? **Solution:** $1,524.
C. Difference over 18 months? **Solution:** ($1,524−$1,124)×18=$7,200.
D. State two alternative explanations for the discount request. **Solution:** Uncertain ROI; budget constraint; risk concerns are also valid.
E. Design a contingent concession with a verifiable condition and approval boundary. **Sample:** A lower price only for a reduced scope with written service terms and authorized approval.
