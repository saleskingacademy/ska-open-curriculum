# Sales — Account Management, Retention and Expansion
Status: DRAFT | Level 5 | Proposed ID SALES-L05-RET-01
Prerequisites: customer onboarding, unit economics, CRM documentation.

## Objectives
Define retention, logo churn, net revenue retention (NRR), expansion and contraction; distinguish customer outcomes from sales activity; calculate retention metrics; design consent-based account reviews and escalation.

## Lesson
An account manager protects customer outcomes after purchase. An **account health** assessment should be grounded in measurable product adoption, delivery quality, support resolution and the customer's own success criteria. Login frequency alone is not a valid proxy for realized value.

**Logo retention** measures customers retained over a period; **logo churn** measures those lost from the starting cohort. **NRR** measures recurring revenue from an existing customer cohort after expansion, contraction and churn, excluding revenue from new customers. State the cohort, measurement period and treatment of one-time fees. NRR may exceed 100% even while some customers churn.

### Worked cohort
At the beginning of a quarter, 40 accounts each pay $500 monthly: starting monthly recurring revenue (MRR) $20,000. During the quarter, four accounts cancel (lost starting MRR $2,000), remaining customers expand by $3,000 MRR and contract by $1,000 MRR. Ending MRR for the original cohort is $20,000−$2,000+$3,000−$1,000=$20,000. NRR=100%. Logo retention=36/40=90%, logo churn=10%. NRR of 100% does not mean every customer was retained.

A **customer success review** should compare actual outcomes against the agreed baseline, acknowledge shortfalls, and identify remediation. Do not pressure a customer to buy expansion features before their core requirements are met. If the customer requests no marketing messages, respect suppression while handling necessary service communications through appropriate permitted channels.

## Failure modes
Misclassifying new customer revenue as expansion; including setup fees in recurring revenue; ignoring cohort definitions; treating complaints as buying signals; concealing implementation failures; incentivizing agents to upsell despite unresolved support issues.

## Exercises
1. Recompute NRR if expansion is $2,000 and contraction $1,500 with all other cohort values unchanged.
2. Describe a health review with adoption, outcomes, unresolved incidents and mutually agreed corrective actions.
3. Explain why 100% NRR can coexist with 10% logo churn.
4. Identify two situations where an upsell should be deferred.

## Quiz and explained answers
Q1. What does NRR exclude? **A:** Revenue from new customers, because it tracks the starting cohort.
Q2. What is starting MRR in the example? **A:** $20,000 (40×$500).
Q3. What is churned starting MRR? **A:** $2,000 (4×$500).
Q4. What is ending cohort MRR? **A:** $20,000 after expansion and contraction.
Q5. What is NRR? **A:** 100%, or $20,000/$20,000.
Q6. What is logo retention? **A:** 90%, or 36/40.
Q7. Does 100% NRR prove no customer churn? **A:** No; expansions may offset lost revenue.
Q8. Is a one-time setup fee part of recurring revenue? **A:** No, because it is nonrecurring.

## Applied examination and solutions
A starting cohort has 60 customers paying $400 MRR each. Six cancel. Remaining customers expand by $4,000 MRR and contract by $1,200 MRR.
A. Starting MRR? **Solution:** 60×$400=$24,000.
B. Churned MRR? **Solution:** 6×$400=$2,400.
C. Ending original-cohort MRR? **Solution:** $24,000−$2,400+$4,000−$1,200=$24,400.
D. NRR? **Solution:** $24,400/$24,000=101.67% (rounded).
E. Logo retention? **Solution:** 54/60=90%.
F. Recommend two account-health interventions not dependent on upselling. **Sample:** resolve a recurring support defect; train users on an underutilized existing workflow.

Rubric: cohort definition 15, calculations 35, interpretation 20, customer outcome plan 20, ethics 10. Pass 80/100 and no material misclassification of new revenue. Reassessment: 50 customers×$300, five cancellations, $1,500 expansion, $500 contraction. Solutions: start $15,000; churn $1,500; ending $14,500; NRR 96.67%; logo retention 90%.
