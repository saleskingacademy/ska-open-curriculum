# Sales Prospect Research, Segmentation and Permission
**Status:** DRAFT. **Level:** 2 of 8. **Unit ID:** SALES-L02-SALES-PROSPECT-RESEARCH-AND-CONTACT. **Purpose:** Human instruction and agent-verifiable competency.

## Entry prerequisites
Demonstrate elementary arithmetic, accurate reading of evidence, basic business vocabulary, and respect for consent and authorized action. For advanced levels, require verified mastery of prior Sales levels, including causal reasoning and quantitative interpretation.

## Learning outcomes
By completion, the learner can explain segmentation, prospect research, lawful contact and suppression; analyze a new case without copying memorized examples; produce an evidence-grounded recommendation; explain material uncertainty; and reject actions beyond legal, privacy or approval boundaries.

## Teaching sequence
**1. Concept formation.** The subject is segmentation, prospect research, lawful contact and suppression. First define the operational terms used in a sales decision, and distinguish observations, hypotheses and proposed actions. A reported customer statement is not an independently verified metric. Record the source and measurement window. A decision is justified only by evidence proportionate to its risk.

**2. Process.** Identify the buyer's stated objective, the actual decision owner, current workflow, costs and constraints. Define what success would look like and how it would be observed. List assumptions separately from facts. Compare at least two feasible alternatives, including declining the opportunity. Calculate relevant economics using contribution rather than mistaking revenue for profit. Seek permission before taking a new action.

**3. Agent implementation.** An agent-readable learning record contains subject_id, level_id, lesson_id, concept_id, prerequisites, source provenance, confidence, canonical answer, accepted alternatives, rubric and version. A task executor additionally checks channel permissions, suppression state, approval authority, pricing bounds and audit logging. No agent may fabricate a buyer response, claim an unverified outcome, or execute an unauthorized contract.

**4. Worked quantitative example.** Of 150 qualified, permissioned contacts, 30 respond and 9 request a meeting. Response rate is 20%; meeting rate per contact is 6%; meeting rate per response is 30%. State the numerator, denominator, period and assumptions. The figures are fictional teaching data. Never treat this example as actual company performance.

**5. Common failure modes.** Confusing activity with outcome; presenting correlation as causality; overlooking costs and timing; failing to document decision authority; repeating stale CRM data; treating automated outreach as permissioned merely because a contact record exists. When information is insufficient, ask for evidence or explicitly abstain.

## Guided exercises with model solutions
1. **Task:** Define a useful market segment without relying on sensitive inferences. **Model solution:** Use role, industry, operational need and explicitly permitted business criteria. **Evaluation:** Award 5 points for a defensible, evidence-grounded response, 3 for an incomplete but nonmisleading response, and 0 for an unsupported or unsafe response.

2. **Task:** Separate public business facts from unverified contact assumptions. **Model solution:** Mark provenance, date and verification status for each field. **Evaluation:** Award 5 points for a defensible, evidence-grounded response, 3 for an incomplete but nonmisleading response, and 0 for an unsupported or unsafe response.

3. **Task:** Design an opt-out suppression check before outreach. **Model solution:** Check suppression and legal channel permissions before every send; stop when prohibited. **Evaluation:** Award 5 points for a defensible, evidence-grounded response, 3 for an incomplete but nonmisleading response, and 0 for an unsupported or unsafe response.

4. **Task:** Explain why scraped contact data does not automatically establish permission. **Model solution:** Availability of data does not establish consent or compliance with applicable law. **Evaluation:** Award 5 points for a defensible, evidence-grounded response, 3 for an incomplete but nonmisleading response, and 0 for an unsupported or unsafe response.

## Eight-question lesson quiz, answer key and rationales
1. **Question:** What is the central subject of this unit? **Answer:** segmentation, prospect research, lawful contact and suppression. **Rationale:** A valid answer must name the unit's decision domain rather than a generic claim about persuasion.
2. **Question:** How should a buyer's unverified estimate be stored? **Answer:** As a reported estimate with source, date and uncertainty. **Rationale:** An estimate cannot be relabeled as audited evidence.
3. **Question:** What must a measurable sales outcome include? **Answer:** Metric, baseline, target, measurement method and period. **Rationale:** These make progress testable.
4. **Question:** Does a higher conversion rate alone prove a sales intervention caused improvement? **Answer:** No. **Rationale:** Selection and confounding may explain the difference.
5. **Question:** What is the appropriate response to an opt-out? **Answer:** Suppress prohibited promotional outreach. **Rationale:** Respect user choice and applicable requirements.
6. **Question:** Can an agent sign a customer contract without delegated authority? **Answer:** No. **Rationale:** Approval must come from an authorized actor.
7. **Question:** Why distinguish contribution from revenue? **Answer:** Direct costs reduce the amount available to cover overhead and profit. **Rationale:** Gross receipts overstate economic value.
8. **Question:** What is required to apply a lesson to an unfamiliar case? **Answer:** New evidence, correct calculation, explicit assumptions and an appropriate decision boundary. **Rationale:** Memorized phrases do not demonstrate transfer.

## Summative assessment (100 points)
**Section A — Case analysis (40 points):** Interpret the worked example above. Identify the inputs and outputs (10), recompute the stated result (10), identify two assumptions (10), and explain a limitation (10). **Answer guide:** Use the quantities and exact results in the worked example; assumptions include stable measurement definitions and accurate observations; causal claims require additional evidence.

**Section B — Applied judgment (40 points):** Complete all four guided exercises above using a new hypothetical customer. Grade 10 each: correct reasoning (5), evidence/provenance (3), clear limits (2). The model solutions above establish the expected reasoning, not a required script.

**Section C — Agent audit (20 points):** Inspect a proposed automated sales action. Identify required consent and suppression checks (5), authorization boundary (5), unsupported claims (5), and a safe next step (5). **Model answer:** Check applicable permission and suppression before contact; do not bind contractual terms without authority; replace unsupported claims with labeled hypotheses; route the next step to an authorized human or permissioned workflow.

**Pass gate:** ≥85/100 overall and ≥16/20 on Section C, with no deliberate fabrication or bypass of an opt-out. A violation triggers remediation even if the numerical score passes.

## Reassessment and retention
After remediation, apply the same reasoning to a distinct account with changed quantities, constraints and buying process. Require the learner to show calculations and provenance without consulting the answer key. Repeat a delayed transfer assessment after a retention interval and compare outcomes; do not claim retention has been measured until tested.

## Graduate and AI-research extension
Develop a preregistered evaluation of segmentation, prospect research, lawful contact and suppression with a clear hypothesis, comparison group, error analysis, robustness checks and reproducible artifacts. Examine distribution shift, stakeholder power and privacy constraints. For AI agents, reserve unseen evaluation cases and test whether they abstain when evidence or authority is missing. These tasks are research extensions, **not** proof that graduate or postdoctoral instruction is complete.
