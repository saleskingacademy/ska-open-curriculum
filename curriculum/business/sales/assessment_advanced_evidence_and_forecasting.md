# Sales Mastery — Module Assessment: Evidence, Qualification, Value, and Forecasting
Assessment ID: SKA-SALES-A05-001
Level: Advanced professional / undergraduate; graduate research extensions explicitly separate.
Prerequisite: Advanced Evidence-Based Deal Diagnosis, consultative discovery, contribution-margin accounting, ethical outreach.

## Competencies
C1 Distinguish observed evidence from inference and unknowns.
C2 Compute and interpret a defensible economic case with uncertainty.
C3 Diagnose multiple causes of opportunity delay.
C4 Design compliant, noncoercive discovery and follow-up.
C5 Construct a calibrated forecast and audit its assumptions.

## Candidate instructions
Time allowance: 90 minutes. Calculator and scratch paper permitted. Do not invent facts absent from a scenario. Identify every assumption. Passing score: at least 80/100 and at least 12/20 on the ethics section. All arithmetic requires units and intermediate steps. ## Case study: the automation proposal
A regional services company reports 50 monthly hours spent reconciling customer inquiries, at a fully loaded labor rate of $38/hour. An automation service costs $1,499 to implement and $2,499 per month. The prospect's operations manager reports that error correction is expensive but has no documented baseline. Procurement requires information-security review. The finance director has not joined the discussion. A seller's CRM assigns an 80% close probability because the operations manager described the demo as impressive. An outreach sequence is scheduled to send three emails, but the prospect has requested no further automated messages.

### Part A: evidence and hypothesis — 20 points
A1 (8): Classify the following as observation, inference, or unknown: the 50-hour report; the asserted 80% probability; the finance director's approval; the procurement security review requirement. Explain each classification.
A2 (6): Propose two plausible, competing reasons the opportunity may stall. For each, specify a question or document that could falsify the hypothesis.
A3 (6): Write a CRM note separating sourced evidence, uncertain claims, and next permitted action. Do not invent a purchase commitment.

### Part B: financial reasoning — 25 points
B1 (7): Calculate the maximum gross monthly labor-time value at the reported rate. Distinguish theoretical capacity from cash savings.
B2 (7): Calculate the first-year subscription and implementation expense assuming 12 full monthly charges and one implementation fee. Exclude taxes and discounts.
B3 (6): Calculate the maximum first-year gross labor-time value assuming the full 50 hours are eliminated each month. Compare it with first-year expense. State whether labor-time value alone supports positive first-year ROI.
B4 (5): Identify three additional measurable outcome categories and the baseline evidence required before counting them as benefits.

### Part C: forecast quality — 20 points
C1 (8): Explain why the CRM's 80% close probability is unsupported. Name four variables that should influence a forecast.
C2 (6): Assume a separate evaluation costs $200 and conditional contribution margin from a win is $2,000. Calculate expected incremental contribution for p=0.10 and p=0.25. Do not confuse this model with the prospect's ROI.
C3 (6): Propose a calibration test using historical opportunities. Explain how to avoid look-ahead bias and why a small sample is uncertain.

### Part D: ethical execution — 20 points
D1 (8): What must happen to the three-email sequence after the prospect's request? Explain the distinction between recording a suppression and merely disabling one scheduled job.
D2 (6): Draft a respectful, permission-based response that does not make an unsupported savings claim and does not send further promotional material.
D3 (6): Explain why the seller must not treat the operations manager's enthusiasm as proof of financial authorization. Identify an ethical way to clarify the buying process.

### Part E: transfer and capstone — 15 points
E1 (8): Draft a proof-of-value protocol with objective, baseline, outcome metric, measurement period, implementation costs, confounders, and stop criteria.
E2 (7): Write a one-paragraph recommendation to the seller choosing proceed, defer, or disqualify. Justify the choice using known evidence and explicit uncertainty.

## Answer key and worked solutions
A1: The 50 hours are a reported observation (not independently measured); 80% is seller inference; finance approval is unknown; security review is a stated procurement requirement. Full credit requires provenance distinctions.
A2: Examples: economic case inadequate (test via finance-approved benefit model); deployment/security risk (test via procurement criteria and technical evidence); authority gap (test via decision-process clarification). Credit falsifiable tests, not generic “follow up.”
A3: Sample: “Operations manager reports 50 hours/month at $38/hour; unverified. Procurement requires security review. Finance approval unknown. Seller's 80% forecast unsupported. Prospect requested no automated outreach; suppress sequence. If permission is obtained through an appropriate channel, clarify whether a documented business case and security review are desired.” Do not contact against the expressed preference.
B1: 50 hours/month × $38/hour = $1,900/month maximum theoretical labor-time value. It is not necessarily realized cash savings; paid staffing costs may remain.
B2: $2,499/month × 12 months = $29,988; plus $1,499 = $31,487 first-year expense.
B3: $1,900/month × 12 = $22,800. Difference: $22,800 − $31,487 = −$8,687. Labor-time value alone is insufficient even under full recovery assumptions.
B4: Possible outcomes: errors avoided (historical error frequency and cost per incident); faster revenue collection (baseline days-sales-outstanding, attributable cash-flow value); incremental conversion (controlled baseline and contribution margin). Do not count unsupported gross sales as profit.
C1: Praise for a demonstration does not establish budget, need, authority, procurement readiness, or implementation feasibility. Forecast should consider stakeholder evidence, approved budget, procurement stages, measured value, historical stage conversion, and forecast horizon.
C2: At p=.10: .10×$2,000−$200=$0. At p=.25: .25×$2,000−$200=$300. This is seller-side expected incremental contribution under assumptions, not customer-side ROI.
C3: Freeze each opportunity's information at its forecast date, then compare predicted probabilities with later actual outcomes. Use reliability bins or a proper scoring rule such as the Brier score. Include lost deals and distinguish cohorts; confidence decreases with small samples.
D1: Cancel scheduled sends and persist a contact-level suppression that every future campaign checks before sending. Record the request and do not attempt circumvention via another agent or sequence.
D2: An acceptable example, only where a response is appropriate and permitted: “I have stopped the automated messages as requested. We will not send further promotional follow-ups.” No upsell or request for re-engagement.
D3: An enthusiastic user may not control budgets or procurement. Ask, only through an appropriate permitted interaction, how financial and technical approval decisions are made; do not fabricate stakeholder endorsement.
E1: Example protocol: objective reduce inquiry reconciliation effort; measure 4-week baseline; compare matched 4-week pilot; metrics staff minutes per case, errors per 100 cases, implementation/support time, and cost; track seasonality and volume; stop for security concerns or failure to achieve pre-agreed outcomes.
E2: A strong recommendation is defer any ROI claim and halt automated outreach; preserve evidence, obtain permission before further contact, and require finance-approved benefit measurement and procurement security review before advancing. Alternate decisions earn credit if evidence-grounded and ethical.
