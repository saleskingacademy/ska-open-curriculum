# Prospect Research — Remediated competency assessment
Status: DRAFT | Level 2 | Companion to sales_prospect_research_and_contact.md

## Instructional correction
Segmentation is a justified grouping of potential buyers by operationally relevant characteristics, not a license to infer sensitive personal attributes. Research quality depends on provenance, recency, business relevance, lawful use, and contact-channel restrictions. An address appearing on the public internet does not automatically create consent to receive automated marketing. Verify applicable law and provider rules before outreach; maintain durable suppression records.

### Worked case
A fictional B2B research team identifies 500 organizations. 320 match a documented industry/size segment; 200 have verified relevant business needs; 120 have documented permission for the proposed channel and no suppression conflicts. Segmentation yield = 320/500=64%; need-fit yield among segmented = 200/320=62.5%; eligible outreach share among need-fit = 120/200=60%; eligible share of original set = 120/500=24%. These figures are descriptive and do not establish permission for any real contact.

### Evidence register
For each candidate, store account ID, role-relevant business criterion, source, observed date, verification method, permissible contact channel, permission basis, suppression status and reviewer. Missing channel permission is a stop condition for workflows that require it. Data minimization means storing only what is needed for the authorized purpose.

## Eight differentiated quiz questions and solutions
1. What proportion of 500 organizations meet the segment definition if 320 qualify? **64%**.
2. Of 320 segmented organizations, what proportion have verified needs if 200 do? **62.5%**.
3. Of 200 need-fit organizations, what proportion are eligible for the specified outreach if 120 are? **60%**.
4. What fraction of the original 500 are eligible? **24%**.
5. A contact record has a source but no observed date. What is missing? **Recency/provenance timestamp**.
6. A company requests suppression of marketing. What must the agent do? **Stop prohibited outreach and persist suppression according to policy and law**.
7. Does a publicly listed email prove marketing consent? **No**; publication and permission are distinct.
8. Why should an agent avoid inferred sensitive traits for prospect ranking? **They may be inaccurate, unnecessary, discriminatory or unlawful**.

## Practical examination (100 points)
A. Recompute the four case percentages and name the denominator for each (40).
B. Produce a six-field minimum evidence record with source, date, need-fit basis, permitted channel, permission basis and suppression status (30; 5 each).
C. Explain three distinct reasons to reject a prospect for the proposed campaign (30; 10 each). **Answer:** no demonstrated fit; inadequate permission or applicable channel restrictions; active suppression or privacy constraint. Other grounded answers accepted.
**Pass:** ≥85/100, plus no attempt to override suppression. **Reassessment:** New dataset: 400 organizations, 240 segment-fit, 150 need-fit, 90 eligible. Results: 60%, 62.5%, 60%, 22.5% respectively.

## Review gates
A legal/compliance reviewer must check jurisdiction-specific channel rules before real outreach. No scraped contact is treated as permission by default. Hold-out learner testing and independent editorial review remain pending.
