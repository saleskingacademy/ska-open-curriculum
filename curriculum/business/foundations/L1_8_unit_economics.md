# Cost Behavior and Unit Economics

**Level:** 1. **Scope:** Additive instructional draft with hypothetical cases; not a certified completed lesson.

## Outcomes
Calculate contribution margin, break-even quantity and customer acquisition payback under explicit assumptions; distinguish unit economics from profitability.

## Instruction
Unit economics evaluates revenue and directly attributable costs per sale, customer or unit. **Contribution margin per unit** equals unit selling price minus unit variable cost. When fixed costs and unit contribution are known, break-even quantity is fixed costs divided by contribution per unit, rounded up to a whole unit. This simplified model assumes stable price, variable cost and relevant operating range.

**Worked case:** A workshop sells tickets at $120. Variable materials and processing cost $30 per attendee. Contribution per attendee is $90. Fixed event costs are $2,250. Break-even quantity is 2,250/90 = **25 attendees**. At 30 attendees, contribution after specified fixed costs is 30 × 90 − 2,250 = $450. This excludes unlisted costs, taxes and refunds.

**Subscription case:** A fictional service collects $100 per month per retained customer. Variable servicing costs are $25 per month, so monthly contribution is $75. If customer acquisition costs $225, simple contribution payback is 225/75 = **3 paid months**. This calculation assumes full payment and stable contribution; it does not establish lifetime value. Churn, discounts, failed collections, onboarding and capital costs may change the result substantially.

**Sensitivity:** If servicing cost rises from $25 to $40, monthly contribution falls to $60 and simple payback becomes 225/60 = 3.75 paid months. A robust decision tests adverse scenarios rather than presenting the best case as guaranteed.

## Exercises and solutions
1. Price $70, variable cost $28, fixed cost $1,680. **Answer:** Contribution $42; break-even 40 units.
2. At 50 units in that scenario, find contribution after fixed costs. **Answer:** 50 × 42 − 1,680 = $420.
3. Monthly revenue $90, variable cost $30, acquisition cost $240. **Answer:** Contribution $60; simple payback four paid months.
4. Why can positive unit contribution coexist with company losses? **Answer:** Total volume may be insufficient to cover fixed costs and omitted expenses.
