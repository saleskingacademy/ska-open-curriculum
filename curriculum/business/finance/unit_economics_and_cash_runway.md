# Business Finance — Unit Economics, Cash Runway and Decision Quality
Status: DRAFT. Level 4–5 bridging intermediate and advanced professional. Proposed ID FIN-L04-UNIT-01.

## Outcomes
Calculate contribution margin, customer acquisition cost (CAC), gross-margin payback, monthly cash burn and runway; distinguish cash from profit; evaluate uncertainty and ethical reporting.

## Lesson
Unit economics describe the revenues and incremental costs attributable to a unit of business activity, often a customer or order. Define the unit and observation horizon explicitly. Revenue is not profit, gross margin is not cash flow, and a positive contribution margin does not guarantee company-level profitability. Fixed costs, taxes, working capital and acquisition spending matter.

### Worked subscription case
A hypothetical business charges $2,499 monthly and $1,499 setup. Assume recurring service delivery costs $800 per month per active customer, setup delivery costs $500, and acquisition cost is $1,200 per new customer. Monthly recurring contribution = $2,499−$800 = $1,699/customer-month. Setup contribution = $1,499−$500 = $999. Initial setup contribution does not necessarily pay acquisition costs immediately: $999−$1,200 = −$201 before recurring contribution. If the first subscription month is collected and earned with the same cost assumptions, combined contribution after acquisition = $999+$1,699−$1,200 = $1,498. These are illustrative, not verified operating costs.

For recurring-only CAC payback, $1,200/$1,699 ≈ 0.71 months, assuming the full monthly recurring contribution is available for payback and excluding setup contribution. Do not confuse this theoretical contribution payback with cash collection timing. If a customer churns after one month, a forecast assuming years of retention is invalid.

### Cash runway
Suppose opening unrestricted cash is $40,000, expected monthly cash receipts are $12,000 and cash payments are $18,000, stable over the period. Net monthly burn is $6,000 and simple runway is $40,000/$6,000 ≈ 6.67 months. This approximation assumes uniform cash timing and no shocks. A 13-week cash-flow forecast should separately schedule actual receipt and payment dates, payroll, taxes, refunds and one-time expenses.

### Sensitivity and governance
If monthly cash payments increase to $20,000 with receipts unchanged, burn becomes $8,000 and simple runway becomes 5 months. Track base, downside and upside cases. Keep deferred revenue and collected cash distinct from earned revenue. State assumptions about churn, refunds, taxes, customer concentration and receivables. Never present hypothetical unit economics as realized revenue.

## Exercises
1. Recompute recurring contribution if delivery cost rises to $1,050/month.
2. With $30,000 cash, $9,000 monthly receipts and $14,000 monthly payments, compute burn and simple runway.
3. Explain why a setup fee collected today may create future service obligations.
4. Draft a downside cash scenario with delayed customer payments and a refund event.

## Quiz with worked answers
Q1. Recurring contribution with $2,499 revenue and $800 direct delivery cost? A: $1,699/month.
Q2. Setup contribution with $1,499 fee and $500 direct cost? A: $999.
Q3. Initial setup contribution less $1,200 acquisition cost? A: −$201.
Q4. First-month combined contribution after CAC? A: $1,498 under stated assumptions.
Q5. Simple burn for $12,000 receipts and $18,000 payments? A: $6,000/month.
Q6. Simple runway with $40,000 cash and $6,000 burn? A: approximately 6.67 months.
Q7. Does positive contribution imply positive company net income? A: No, fixed costs and other expenses remain.
Q8. Why might actual runway differ from simple runway? A: Cash timing, changing burn, one-time expenses and revenue variability.

## Applied assessment and rubric
Case: opening cash $52,000; monthly receipts $16,000; monthly payments $23,500. Compute net burn ($7,500/month), simple runway (6.93 months), and downside runway if receipts fall by $3,000 (burn $10,500, runway 4.95 months). Write a one-page recommendation distinguishing actions that increase cash, improve margin or merely defer expense. Grade 100 points: arithmetic 30, assumptions 20, scenario comparison 20, cash/profit distinction 15, responsible recommendation 15. Pass 80, reassess using new values.
