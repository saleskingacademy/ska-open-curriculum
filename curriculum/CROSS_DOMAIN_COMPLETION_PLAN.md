# Mathematics, business, sales and engineering completion programme

Status: implementation plan; subject completion remains evidence-based.
Governing policy: [coverage-based completion](../standards/COVERAGE_BASED_COMPLETION_POLICY.md), [zero-to-frontier standard](../standards/ZERO_TO_FRONTIER_CURRICULUM_STANDARD.md), and [assessment standard](../standards/ACADEMIC_EQUIVALENCE_AND_ASSESSMENT_STANDARD.md).

The repository already has 369 grouped catalogue subjects plus finer topics and expanded mathematics specializations. That is source breadth, not 369 finished courses. Preserve those sources and audit each entry before counting it as a complete lesson package. The programme inventory reports all grouped subjects so “engineering,” “business” and “everything else” do not become invisible promises.

## Execution spine and parallel study paths

| Path | Foundation | Core sequence | Advanced branches and completion evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | Counting, Arithmetic, Pre-Algebra | Algebra, geometry, trigonometry, precalculus, calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, discrete mathematics and probability/statistics | Analysis, algebra, topology, numerical/optimization, graduate specializations, SAT/SMT/formal/AI mathematics; proofs, models, code and independent validation |
| Business | Value exchange, customers, resources, cash records | Accounting, economics, operations, marketing, entrepreneurship, management, finance, strategy and organizational behavior | Decision models, analytics, governance, global operations, responsible automation; cases, budgets, operational projects and capstone |
| Sales | Buyer need, fit, discovery, evidence, truthful commitments | Prospecting, communication, qualification, demonstrations, objections, negotiation, closing, handoff, retention and account management | Enterprise buying, sales leadership, revenue operations, forecasting, pricing, experiments and verified AI assistance; roleplays, observed decisions and cases |
| Engineering | Systems, units, measurement, assumptions, requirements and verification | Algebra/trigonometry/calculus, physics, chemistry where applicable, computing, drawing/CAD, materials, mechanics, circuits, thermodynamics, fluids and design | Mechanical, electrical/power, civil/structural, chemical/petroleum, aerospace, biomedical, environmental, industrial/systems, nuclear, mining, agricultural, marine/ocean, manufacturing and materials; labs, models, validated design work |
| Supporting disciplines | Literacy, study skills, critical thinking and communication | Research/source literacy, programming, data, cybersecurity, ethics, technical writing and project methods | Cross-domain implementations and research literacy; source-grounded evaluation and reproducibility |

Arrows in the mathematics master plan indicate dependency, not an obligation to finish every branch before any introductory business study. For example, a whole-number cash record needs addition/subtraction; financial modeling later needs percentages and algebra. Elementary engineering measurement can begin before calculus, but calculus-based mechanics cannot assume that entry lesson supplied its mathematics.

## Starter course maps

These maps are small declared entry courses, not substitutes for the complete fields. Numbered lessons without source files are planned only. Their lessons should expand the audited legacy source, and all final/module assessment gates must close before course completion.

Introduction to Business, 12 planned foundation lessons: (1) value exchange and operating records; (2) customer problems and research; (3) offer/value proposition; (4) resources and operating processes; (5) revenue, expenses and profit; (6) cash timing and working capital; (7) basic accounting records; (8) cost behavior and unit economics; (9) marketing and sales coordination; (10) service delivery and quality; (11) decision evidence, stakeholders and risk; (12) integrated business case. Prerequisites progress from Arithmetic addition/subtraction to fractions/percentages and basic algebra where needed. Later specialist courses deepen finance, management and strategy.

Professional Selling, 12 planned foundation lessons: (1) discovery, fit and evidence; (2) buyer/customer distinctions and buying context; (3) prospect research and relevance; (4) permission and opening communication; (5) discovery questions and listening; (6) qualification with documented uncertainty; (7) value explanation and demonstration; (8) objections as unresolved information; (9) negotiation and tradeoffs; (10) explicit agreement and next steps; (11) delivery handoff and follow-up; (12) observed sales case and ethical roleplay. Quantitative value and forecasts require validated math prerequisites, explicit assumptions and independent checks.

Engineering Fundamentals, 12 planned foundation lessons: (1) systems, units and verification; (2) measurement and uncertainty; (3) requirements and constraints; (4) diagrams, drawings and interfaces; (5) mathematical models and assumptions; (6) forces and equilibrium after algebra/vector foundations; (7) motion and energy after physics prerequisites; (8) materials and failure concepts; (9) electrical quantities and simple circuits; (10) design alternatives and tradeoffs; (11) test planning and reproducibility; (12) low-risk design investigation. No introductory lesson certifies a safety-critical physical design or replaces professional requirements.

## Assessment obligations

Each entry course needs lesson quizzes and explanatory keys, meaningful independent work, module/cumulative tests with outcome blueprints, final examination, alternate reassessment, a scored case or project and prerequisite remediation. Advanced paths add discipline-specific proofs, laboratory/modeling evidence, implementation, uncertainty analysis, literature methods and capstone. Do not label a list of future lessons “instructionally populated.”
