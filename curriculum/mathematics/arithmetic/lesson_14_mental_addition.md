## Entry check and learning outcomes

Explain 37 as thirty and seven, derive eight plus seven using ten, and calculate 46 + 28 using columns. Entry responses: 37 = 30 + 7; eight plus two plus five is fifteen; 46 + 28 = 74. Review Lessons 3, 12 and 13 if those explanations are uncertain. Fast recall is useful but is not an entry requirement.

By the end, choose and explain a mental strategy, partition an addend without losing quantity, build a convenient ten or hundred, regroup several addends, distinguish exact transformations from estimates, and verify an answer through a different representation. These outcomes concern nonnegative whole numbers. Signed arithmetic and decimal procedures have their own later lessons.

## Vocabulary and first principles

A **partition** splits a quantity into parts whose sum is the original quantity. A **friendly total** is convenient for the learner, often a multiple of ten or one hundred. An **exact transformation** preserves the answer. An **estimate** deliberately changes the question to obtain an approximate scale. A **running total** records how much has already been included.

Mental arithmetic is reasoning with quantity, not a requirement to hold every intermediate result silently. A learner may write checkpoints, explain aloud, use tactile counters or draw jumps. The mathematical goal is to select useful structure and account for every part exactly once. Accessibility supports do not lower that goal.

Imagine 48 counters in one tray and 27 in another. Move two counters from the second tray into the first. Now the trays hold 50 and 25. No counters were created or discarded, so both arrangements have the same total, 75. This is the central idea of compensation by transfer. The easier problem is equivalent because one quantity increases by exactly what the other decreases.

## Why the strategies work

Addition permits changing order and grouping. Changing order is commutativity: a + b = b + a. Changing grouping is associativity: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c). Parentheses identify which part to combine first; these laws explain why a convenient grouping preserves the sum.

If an addend is already split into two parts, a + (b + c) may be calculated as (a + b) + c. For example, 46 + 28 becomes 46 + (20 + 8), then 66 + 8 = 74. Another partition uses 28 = 4 + 24, giving (46 + 4) + 24 = 74. Both decompositions preserve twenty-eight. Merely adding four to forty-six while keeping all twenty-eight would count four extra units.

For this lesson, transfer never takes more counters than the donor tray contains. This keeps every intermediate quantity a whole number. Later algebra generalizes the rule using subtraction and signed numbers. Here the concrete partition is sufficient: if the second tray contains k + r counters, a + (k + r) = (a + k) + r.

Exact mental methods and rounding have different purposes. Replacing 48 and 27 by 50 and 30 gives about 80. That can check scale, but it is not the exact total 75. Transferring two gives 50 and 25 and preserves the total exactly. Always state which kind of operation you performed.

## Worked examples

**E1 — partition by place.** For 63 + 24, add twenty to sixty-three to obtain eighty-three, then four to obtain eighty-seven. Record 63 + 24 = 63 + (20 + 4) = 83 + 4 = 87. Check with place contributions: sixty plus twenty is eighty, and three plus four is seven.

**E2 — reach a ten.** For 58 + 16, split sixteen into two and fourteen. Two brings fifty-eight to sixty; fourteen brings sixty to seventy-four. Thus 58 + 16 = 60 + 14 = 74. Verify with 58 + 10 = 68 and six more gives 74.

**E3 — reach a hundred.** For 297 + 46, move three of the forty-six units to the first quantity. The remaining part is forty-three, since three plus forty-three is forty-six. Then 300 + 43 = 343. Check by columns: ones thirteen, tens fourteen including carry, hundreds three including carry.

**E4 — group convenient pairs.** For 25 + 38 + 75 + 62, pair twenty-five with seventy-five and thirty-eight with sixty-two. Each pair is one hundred, so the total is two hundred. The justification uses both changing order and changing grouping. Each original addend appears once.

**E5 — choose not to force compensation.** For 432 + 256, place partition is already convenient: 432 + 200 = 632; forty? No: the remaining quantity is fifty-six, so add fifty to reach 682, then six to reach 688. Deliberately checking the remaining partition prevents a fluent but incorrect checkpoint. Verify 400 + 200 + 30 + 50 + 2 + 6 = 688.

**E6 — hypothetical stock receipts.** Three distinct deliveries contain 199, 304 and 101 units. Pair 199 with 101 to make 300, then include 304 for 604 units. The numerical shortcut is valid only after confirming that the deliveries are distinct and measured in the same unit. It does not establish that a real inventory record is accurate.

## Guided practice

G1. Complete the partition for 69 + 15 by reaching seventy. **Response:** Fifteen is one plus fourteen. Seventy plus fourteen is eighty-four.

G2. Calculate 248 + 32 by reaching 250. **Response:** Thirty-two is two plus thirty, so the sum is 250 + 30 = 280.

G3. Explain 14 + 27 + 6. **Response:** Fourteen and six make twenty; adding twenty-seven gives forty-seven. Reordering did not omit any addend.

G4. Is 398 + 7 best treated as 400 + 7? **Response:** No. Two of the seven units were used to reach 400, leaving five. Correct equivalent problem: 400 + 5 = 405.

## Transfer, misconceptions and edge cases

T1. A learner says 68 + 27 = 70 + 27 = 97. Diagnose the first invalid equality. **Solution:** The first equality creates two units. Split twenty-seven into two and twenty-five, giving 70 + 25 = 95. The second calculation, 70 + 27 = 97, is correct for the wrong transformed input. Separate the modeling mistake from the subsequent arithmetic.

T2. One tray holds 98 counters and the other holds one. Can the learner transfer two from the second tray to make one hundred? **Solution:** Not within this whole-number tray model. The donor has only one counter. Combine them directly to obtain 99. A later signed expression could represent a different transformation, but it is not this physical transfer.

T3. A simulation has three distinct request batches of 498, 502 and 37. Report the exact combined count and an approximate scale. **Solution:** The first two batches make 1,000; the exact total is 1,037 requests. Roughly one thousand describes scale. Neither result measures processing time or verifies any production system.

Do not infer that one mental method is always fastest. Efficiency depends on the operands, familiarity, working-memory demands and error risk. Do not silently replace missing inputs by zero. Do not combine unlike units such as three boxes and four individual items unless the box contents are known. These are input-model issues, not failures of addition.

## Computational and AI-augmented practice

Construct a three-column trace: original addends, rewritten addends, preserved total. For 297 + 46, write 300 + 43 and verify that the moved three is included once. A program could compare both totals for many examples, but testing a finite sample alone does not prove a rule for all whole numbers. The partition argument explains why the rule holds in the stated domain.

Audit this constructed machine response: “Add ten to both addends to make them friendly; the sum stays the same.” Use 21 + 34 = 55 as a counterexample: 31 + 44 = 75. Increasing both addends by ten increases the total by twenty. Agreement between two AI outputs would not fix the invalid rule. Ask for a derivation and check the conserved quantity yourself.

Optional future bridge: binary arithmetic also relies on place value, but uses groups of two. Do not assume that a decimal-friendly transformation is the best binary algorithm. This lesson establishes preservation of quantity; Lessons 61–62 introduce the representation change explicitly.

## Formative quiz and alternate reassessment

Each quiz item is worth two points: one for the answer or judgment, one for a valid explanation.

Q1. 88 + 17.
Q2. 396 + 28.
Q3. 45 + 19 + 55.
Q4. Is replacing 49 + 21 by 50 + 20 exact or approximate?
Q5. Why is replacing 49 + 21 by 50 + 21 invalid as an equality?

Answers: Q1 105, from 90 + 15. Q2 424, from 400 + 24. Q3 119, from 100 + 19. Q4 exact, because one unit transfers between addends. Q5 it adds an extra unit; the new total is 71 rather than 70.

Alternate form: R1 78 + 25; R2 598 + 34; R3 35 + 18 + 65; R4 classify 79 + 31 = 80 + 30; R5 diagnose 79 + 31 = 80 + 31. Answers: 103; 632; 118; exact transfer; invalid increase of one, changing 110 to 111. Require explanations under the same rubric.

## Mastery, remediation and retrieval

Require at least nine of ten independent answers correct, 8/10 quiz points, and both correct result and quantity-preservation explanation on a new transfer problem. Correct every lost or duplicated contribution before proceeding. These are local mastery gates, not evidence that the full Arithmetic course is complete.

If partitions fail, reconstruct each addend with counters and verify that its pieces recombine. If regrouping fails, mark each addend once after use. If exact and approximate results are confused, label every transformation as preserving or changing the original problem. Retry the alternate quiz with new work rather than memorizing the first form.

Retrieval cues: preserve the total; partition before moving; use every part once; distinguish exact from approximate; choose a check that changes the representation. Continue to the meaning of subtraction once these explanations are reliable.
