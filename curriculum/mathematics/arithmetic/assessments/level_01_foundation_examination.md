# Level 1 foundation examination: Forms A and B

Scope: Arithmetic Lessons 1–10. This is a module and cumulative foundation assessment, not the comprehensive course final. Lessons 1–5 use preserved original explanations together with their structured companions. Issuing this examination does not by itself certify the module's instructional completeness or independent academic validation.

## Administration, blueprint, and scoring

Suggested workload: 45–75 minutes per form, plus a separate performance task. Timing is guidance, not a pass condition. Allow paper, counters, tactile lines, or equivalent accessible materials. Read prompts aloud when needed. Preserve numerical and reasoning demands. No calculator or AI during independent testing. Tools can check work after submission and should be labeled as later verification.

Each form has 25 items, two points each, total 50. Award one point for the correct result or interpretation and one for the requested explanation, representation, or verification. For a two-part item, both parts are needed for the result point; award partial explanation credit only if the correct principle is evident. Do not award the reasoning point for restating the answer. Equivalent clear reasoning is acceptable.

| Outcome | Lessons | Items in both forms | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1: Count, cardinal/ordinal distinction, count integrity | 1–2 | 1–4 | Recall, procedure, error analysis |
| F2: Numerals, zero placeholders, place value | 2–3 | 5–8 | Interpretation and representation |
| F3: Comparison, number lines, sign and distance | 4–6 | 9–13 | Order, application, interval reasoning |
| F4: Estimation, justified bounds, uncertainty | 7 | 14–17 | Modeling and decisions |
| F5: Rounding, ties, carry, capacity | 8 | 18–21 | Procedure and transfer |
| F6: Statements, endpoints, equality, justification | 9–10 | 22–25 | Logic, diagnosis and synthesis |

Critical outcomes are F1 and F2. Require at least 80% overall (40/50), at least 90% across F1+F2 (15/16 after rounding the threshold upward to an attainable score), and no other outcome below 70%: F3 at least 7/10; F4, F5, F6 each at least 6/8. Correct every failed prerequisite item before advancement. The separate performance task uses Lesson 10's five-dimension project rubric and requires 8/10 with full count-integrity and verification credit. Routine lesson sets retain the stricter 85% rule.

## Form A: learner questions

A1. Count the marks in `● ● ● ● ● ● ● ●`. Explain a method that prevents counting one twice.
A2. In “the fifth learner holds five cards,” identify the ordinal and cardinal uses.
A3. A set of twelve counters is rearranged without adding or removing any. What is its new count, and why?
A4. A process visits every integer label from 0 through 9 inclusive once. How many labels are visited? Explain the endpoint issue.
A5. Distinguish a number from a numeral using the value seven.
A6. Expand 40,507 by place value and explain its tens zero.
A7. State the value of 8 in 18,204 and name its place.
A8. Explain why 3.40 and 3.4 are equal, but 3.04 is different.
A9. Compare 45,901 and 45,910, explaining the first unequal place.
A10. Compare 0.507 and 0.57 using trailing zeros or a place-value chart.
A11. Order −2, 4, −7, 0, and 3 from least to greatest.
A12. State both position and distance from zero for a marker at −6.
A13. Find the separation from −3 to 4 by counting unit intervals.
A14. Estimate four groups of approximately 20 objects. State that the result is approximate.
A15. Seven complete rows contain ten tokens each and a last row contains one to nine. Give inclusive count bounds.
A16. Using A15, are 80 spaces guaranteed enough? Are 75? Explain both.
A17. An estimate is 50; an exact recount is 47. Find the error size and high/low direction.
A18. Round 3,476 to tens and to hundreds under nonnegative nearest, ties upward.
A19. Round 650 to hundreds under the same rule, identifying the tie.
A20. Round 9.96 to tenths and explain the carry.
A21. A known count is 241 and capacity is available in tens. Give the smallest sufficient capacity and explain why nearest rounding is not the capacity rule.
A22. Translate “a whole count of at most twelve,” including the domain.
A23. List whole counts satisfying 2 ≤ N < 5, explaining endpoints.
A24. Repair 3 + 4 = 7 + 2 = 9 using valid equations; explain the false link.
A25. A record says “approximately 100 objects; therefore exactly 100 objects.” Identify the unsupported inference and state what information would settle the exact count.

## Form A explanatory solutions

A1. Eight; match each mark to one count word and mark or isolate counted objects.
A2. “Fifth” is ordinal position; “five” is cardinal quantity.
A3. Twelve; rearranging without addition/removal preserves the collection's size.
A4. Ten; zero is itself a visited label, so count 0, 1, ..., 9 rather than only the ending label.
A5. Seven is the quantity/value; 7, seven, or VII are possible representations of it.
A6. 40,000 + 500 + 7. The tens zero prevents 7 from occupying tens; the thousands zero also preserves place.
A7. 8,000, the thousands place.
A8. An extra zero hundredths contributes zero; in 3.04 the four contributes hundredths rather than tenths.
A9. 45,901 < 45,910; tens differ first after equal higher places, with 0 < 1.
A10. 0.507 < 0.570; hundredths 0 < 7 after equal tenths.
A11. −7, −2, 0, 3, 4 by left-to-right numerical order.
A12. Position −6; distance 6 units, nonnegative.
A13. Seven units, three to zero and four onward.
A14. About 80 objects; four comparable groups of about twenty, not an exact count.
A15. 71 through 79 inclusive; seventy in full rows plus at least one and at most nine.
A16. Eighty guarantees sufficiency because 79 fits; seventy-five does not because 76–79 remain possible.
A17. Three objects; the estimate was high because 50 > 47.
A18. 3,480 and 3,500; ones 6 and tens 7 respectively select upper neighbors.
A19. 700; 650 is halfway between 600 and 700 and the declared tie rule selects the latter.
A20. 10.0; 9.96 exceeds midpoint 9.95 and increasing nine tenths carries into the ones place.
A21. 250; 240 cannot hold 241. Nearest rounding would give 240, which is insufficient.
A22. N ≤ 12 with N a nonnegative whole count; twelve is allowed.
A23. 2, 3, 4; include two, exclude five.
A24. 3 + 4 = 7; then 7 + 2 = 9. The original first equality compares values seven and nine as equal.
A25. Approximation does not prove exactness. Obtain an exact count or another independently justified determination of the count.

## Form B: alternate/reassessment questions

Use the same administration, outcome groups, and scoring. Do not show this key before independent work. Alternate items vary values and context; they are not evidence that a learner can handle every possible task in the outcome.

B1. Count `● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ●` and explain how you avoid skipping a mark.
B2. In “the fourth shelf contains four bowls,” identify ordinal and cardinal meanings.
B3. A collection of seventeen buttons is spread farther apart without changing its membership. What is its count and why?
B4. Every integer label from 0 through 12 inclusive is inspected once. How many inspections occur, and why?
B5. Explain how “nine” and 9 can represent the same number without being the same written symbol.
B6. Expand 70,306 and explain the tens zero.
B7. State the value and place of 6 in 26,105.
B8. Explain why 2.50 equals 2.5 but differs from 2.05.
B9. Compare 62,809 and 62,890, showing the first unequal place.
B10. Compare 0.406 and 0.46 using aligned places.
B11. Order −5, 1, −9, 0, and 6.
B12. State position and distance from zero for −8.
B13. Find separation from −4 to 5 using intervals.
B14. Estimate three groups of approximately 30 objects and label the estimate.
B15. Six complete rows contain ten tokens each and a last row contains one to nine. Give inclusive bounds.
B16. For B15, are 70 spaces guaranteed enough? Are 65? Explain.
B17. An estimate is 60; exact count is 64. Find error size and high/low direction.
B18. Round 5,683 to tens and hundreds under nonnegative nearest, ties upward.
B19. Round 750 to hundreds and explain the tie decision.
B20. Round 8.97 to tenths and explain the carry.
B21. A known count is 321 and capacity is offered in tens. Give smallest sufficient capacity and compare it with nearest rounding.
B22. Translate “a whole count of at least fourteen,” including its domain.
B23. List whole counts satisfying 3 < N ≤ 6 and explain endpoints.
B24. Repair 2 + 5 = 7 + 1 = 8 into valid equations, explaining the false link.
B25. A record has no observed count, but software records zero. Explain why that can misrepresent information and state how to distinguish the cases.

## Form B explanatory solutions

B1. Ten; match one count word per mark and track counted marks.
B2. Fourth is ordinal position; four is cardinal bowl count.
B3. Seventeen, since spacing changes layout but not membership or quantity.
B4. Thirteen; the inspected label zero counts as an inspection.
B5. Both denote the value nine under their conventions; one uses words, the other a decimal digit.
B6. 70,000 + 300 + 6; the tens zero locates six in ones, and the thousands zero preserves higher spacing.
B7. 6,000 in thousands.
B8. Zero hundredths adds nothing; 2.05 has five hundredths instead of five tenths.
B9. 62,809 < 62,890; tens differ first, 0 < 9.
B10. 0.406 < 0.460; hundredths 0 < 6.
B11. −9, −5, 0, 1, 6 by signed order.
B12. Position −8; distance 8 units.
B13. Nine units, four to zero and five beyond.
B14. About 90 objects, based on three approximate groups of thirty.
B15. 61 through 69 inclusive; sixty plus at least one and at most nine.
B16. Seventy guarantees capacity; sixty-five does not because counts 66–69 remain possible.
B17. Four objects; estimate low because 60 < 64.
B18. 5,680 and 5,700; ones 3 retains tens, tens 8 increases hundreds.
B19. 800; exact midpoint between 700 and 800, tie upward.
B20. 9.0; 8.97 exceeds midpoint 8.95, with carry through tenths.
B21. 330; nearest gives 320, which is too small for 321.
B22. N ≥ 14, with N a nonnegative whole count. Fourteen is included.
B23. 4, 5, 6; three excluded and six included.
B24. 2 + 5 = 7; then 7 + 1 = 8. Seven and eight are not equal.
B25. Missing information does not assert an empty collection. Preserve “not observed/unknown” separately from a confirmed exact zero.

## Remediation routes and score record

Record each outcome score, failed item IDs, explanation errors, corrective work, alternate-form scores, and the project evidence. Overall success cannot compensate for failing a critical outcome.

F1 failures: return to counting and ordinal/cardinal examples in Lesson 1; count the same collection in two arrangements with one-to-one tracking. F2: revisit Lessons 2–3 using numeral/quantity pairs and place-value charts with internal zeros. F3: draw or build a signed number line from −10 to 10; compare pairs and count gaps, returning to Lessons 4–6. F4: work Lesson 7's bound/capacity cases and distinguish justified endpoints from guesses. F5: use Lesson 8's neighbor/midpoint method, tie/carry cases, and exact-count capacity comparisons. F6: translate Lesson 9's conditions in both directions and repair invalid equality chains.

After targeted practice, reassess the failed outcomes using Form B without the key. If another form is needed later, author fresh coherent tasks at the same outcome demands and verify the answers before use. Preserve an independent mixed task after remediation so narrowly practiced answers do not masquerade as transfer. Complete the Lesson 10 project separately. No result from this examination should be labeled course accreditation, institutional credit, or complete Arithmetic mastery.
