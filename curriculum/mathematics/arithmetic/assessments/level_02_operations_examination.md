# Level 2: Addition and subtraction cumulative examination

Canonical ID: AR-L02-EXAM. Scope: Lessons 11–20, with Level 1 prerequisites.
Status: authored public self-study assessment with keys; not a secure credentialing bank.
Return: [Lesson 20](../lesson_20_operations_mastery.md) and [reading order](../README.md).

## Administration and assessment blueprint

Choose Form A for the first attempt and Form B for reassessment after remediation. Work independently before consulting the public key. Both forms test the same outcomes with different numbers and contexts. Explanations may be written, spoken or represented with diagrams/tactile models. Do not impose a speed cutoff as a substitute for understanding. Calculators and AI are allowed only for a separately labeled post-attempt audit, not the independent score.

| Items on each form | Outcome | Evidence | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Addition meaning, facts, multi-digit and mental methods | Exact result plus decomposition/carry/part-whole explanation | 10 |
| 6–10 | Subtraction meaning and place-value exchanges | Exact result plus inverse check or exchange trace | 10 |
| 11–15 | Signed arithmetic and interpretation | Exact result plus zero-pair, number-line or opposite explanation | 10 |
| 16–20 | Estimates, bounds and checking | Result/judgment plus a justified error bound or check limitation | 10 |
| 21–25 | Modeling, reconciliation, transfer and error diagnosis | Defensible conclusion plus model/assumptions | 10 |

For each item, award one point for the correct result or judgment and one for sound reasoning. A correct number with invalid reasoning earns at most one. For tasks with multiple required numerical outputs, the result point requires all outputs correct. A minor arithmetic slip with a valid method may retain the reasoning point. A wrong model does not earn reasoning credit because a calculator correctly evaluated it.

Mastery requires at least 40/50 overall, at least 34/40 across Items 1–20 (85% routine/checking work), at least 8/10 across Items 21–25 (80% transfer), and at least 9/10 in each prerequisite-critical domain: addition, subtraction and signed operations. Correct all missed prerequisite skills before progressing. Retake after targeted instruction with the unused form; do not combine best individual answers from different attempts into one passing score. These criteria are SKA learning gates, not an accreditor's certification rules.

## Form A — learner paper

1. Calculate 386 + 247; explain a carry.
2. Calculate 498 + 36 with an exact mental transformation.
3. Calculate 125 + 64 + 75; explain regrouping.
4. A set has seven red counters and five blue counters, with no overlap. Find the total and identify the parts and whole.
5. Calculate 2,009 + 87 and explain placement of the shorter addend.
6. Calculate 72 − 46; show the exchange.
7. Calculate 600 − 287; explain passage through zero.
8. Calculate 4,002 − 89; check by addition.
9. One collection contains twenty-three cards and another contains sixteen. How many more are in the first?
10. Calculate 8,000 − 8,000 and explain the zero result.
11. Calculate −14 + 9.
12. Calculate −8 + (−6).
13. Calculate 7 − (−12).
14. Calculate −13 − (−5).
15. A signed position changes from −6 to 4. Calculate final minus initial and interpret the sign.
16. Calculate 397 + 208 exactly and estimate it by nearest tens; state the estimate's absolute error.
17. Calculate 802 − 396 exactly and estimate it by nearest tens; state the absolute error.
18. Each of three inputs was rounded to the nearest ten. What bound follows for the sum's absolute rounding error?
19. If 30 ≤ a ≤ 34 and 8 ≤ b ≤ 11, give the smallest and largest possible values of a − b.
20. Two reports have the same total. Does that prove their individual input records agree? Explain or give a counterexample.
21. Begin with 735 items, receive 148, ship 269 and receive 17 approved returned items. Find ending stock and write a reconciliation equation. Assume distinct events and no other changes.
22. For Item 21, a timed independent count is 628. Calculate observed minus calculated. Does the discrepancy identify a unique cause?
23. A simulated signed balance begins at −24, increases by 39 and decreases by 18. Give the intermediate and final balances.
24. Diagnose: “53 − 28 = 35 because subtract the smaller digit from the larger in each column.” Give the correct answer and the first invalid rule.
25. A learner evaluates −7 − (−4) as −11. Explain the sign error, correct the result and provide an inverse check.

## Remediation and progression record

Record scores by outcome domain and distinguish calculation, model, sign, unit, checking and explanation errors. Keep actual learner identities, attempts and grades in the private learner system, never in this public repository. After review, the learner corrects the missed items with a second representation, solves fresh related tasks and then attempts the alternate form independently.

Addition gaps return to Lessons 11–14; subtraction gaps to 15–17; signed gaps to 18; estimation and evidence gaps to 19; ledger/modeling gaps to 20. The module investigation in Lesson 20 must separately meet its rubric. Passing these public practice materials establishes only the stated local learning evidence; independent academic review, secure credentialing assessment and the remainder of the Arithmetic course remain separate requirements.
