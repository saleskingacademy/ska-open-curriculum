SALES PSYCHOLOGY — COMPLETE CURRICULUM

FOUNDATIONS: Sales psychology is the study of why people buy — the mental and emotional processes behind purchase decisions. Core principle: humans make decisions emotionally and justify them rationally. The neuroscience: the limbic system (emotion) decides, the neocortex (logic) rationalizes. Selling to logic alone fails; you must reach the emotional driver, then arm the buyer with logic to defend the choice.

CIALDINI'S 6 PRINCIPLES OF INFLUENCE: 1) Reciprocity — people feel obligated to return favors; give value first (insight, free trial, help). 2) Commitment & Consistency — people align actions with prior commitments; get small yeses that build to the big one. 3) Social Proof — people follow others like them; use testimonials, case studies, "companies like yours." 4) Authority — people defer to credible experts; demonstrate expertise and credentials. 5) Liking — people buy from those they like and who are similar; build genuine rapport. 6) Scarcity — people value what's limited; legitimate urgency and exclusivity. (7th, later added: Unity — shared identity/tribe.)

LOSS AVERSION & PROSPECT THEORY (Kahneman & Tversky): The pain of losing is roughly 2x the pleasure of gaining an equal amount. Frame offerings around what the buyer loses by not acting (cost of inaction, competitive disadvantage, ongoing pain) — more powerful than potential gains. Reference points and anchoring shape perceived value.

COGNITIVE BIASES IN BUYING: Anchoring (first number sets the frame — present premium option first). Framing effect (same info, different presentation changes decisions). Bandwagon effect (popularity drives adoption). Sunk cost fallacy (past investment justifies continuation). Confirmation bias (buyers seek info supporting their leaning). Decoy effect (a third option makes another look better). Status quo bias (inertia favors doing nothing — your biggest enemy).

EMOTIONAL DRIVERS: The six core buying emotions — fear (of loss, of missing out, of making a mistake), greed (gain, advantage, status), pride (recognition, ego), guilt, hope, and belonging. Every purchase serves an emotional need beneath the stated logical reason. Uncover the emotional "why."

TRUST & RAPPORT: Trust = Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy, divided by Self-Orientation (Trust Equation, Maister). Mirroring (matching pace, tone, body language), active listening, and labeling emotions ("It sounds like you're frustrated with...") build connection. Vulnerability and honesty accelerate trust faster than polish.

QUESTIONING PSYCHOLOGY: Questions that make buyers articulate their own pain create ownership of the problem — self-persuasion is stronger than being told. The Socratic method leads buyers to conclusions they believe are their own. Calibrated questions ("How am I supposed to do that?") shift dynamics without confrontation (Chris Voss).

PERSUASION vs MANIPULATION: Ethical persuasion aligns the buyer's genuine interest with your solution — win-win. Manipulation exploits bias against the buyer's interest — short-term win, long-term reputation destruction. Master sellers persuade ethically: they'd recommend the same choice if they were the buyer.

OBJECTION PSYCHOLOGY: Objections are emotional before logical. Behind "too expensive" is often fear of making a mistake or lack of perceived value. Behind "need to think" is unspoken concern or missing stakeholder. Address the emotion beneath the words, not just the surface statement.

MASTERY LEVELS: L1 — recognize emotional vs logical buying. L2-3 — apply influence principles and read buyer psychology. L4-5 — navigate bias and emotional drivers in complex deals. L6-7 — orchestrate multi-stakeholder psychology. L8 Grandmaster — teach persuasion ethics and build psychologically-informed sales cultures.
