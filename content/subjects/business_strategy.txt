BUSINESS STRATEGY — COMPLETE CURRICULUM

FOUNDATIONS: Strategy is the set of choices about where to play and how to win that create sustainable competitive advantage. It is fundamentally about trade-offs — deciding what NOT to do. Strategy answers: What business are we in? Who are our customers? What unique value do we deliver? Why will we win and keep winning?

PORTER'S FIVE FORCES: Industry attractiveness is shaped by — 1) Threat of new entrants (barriers to entry). 2) Bargaining power of suppliers. 3) Bargaining power of buyers. 4) Threat of substitutes. 5) Competitive rivalry. Analyze these to understand profit potential and position accordingly. Strong forces compress margins; weak forces enable profit.

COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE (Porter's Generic Strategies): Three paths — 1) Cost Leadership (be the lowest-cost producer). 2) Differentiation (offer unique value worth a premium). 3) Focus (dominate a narrow niche via cost or differentiation). The fatal error is being "stuck in the middle" — neither cheapest nor most distinctive.

SWOT & POSITIONING: Strengths and Weaknesses (internal), Opportunities and Threats (external). Position where your strengths meet market opportunity and competitors are weak. Strategic positioning is about being different, not just better — occupying a distinct space in the customer's mind.

VALUE CHAIN & CORE COMPETENCIES: The value chain maps every activity that adds value (inbound logistics, operations, marketing, service, plus support activities). Competitive advantage comes from performing key activities better or cheaper. Core competencies — the unique capabilities hard for rivals to copy — are the true foundation of advantage.

BLUE OCEAN STRATEGY: Instead of competing in bloody "red oceans" of existing demand, create uncontested "blue ocean" market space. Value innovation — simultaneously pursue differentiation AND low cost by eliminating/reducing industry factors while raising/creating new ones. Make competition irrelevant.

GROWTH STRATEGIES (Ansoff Matrix): Market Penetration (more of existing product to existing market), Market Development (existing product, new markets), Product Development (new product, existing market), Diversification (new product, new market — highest risk). Choose based on capability and risk appetite.

BUSINESS MODELS & MOATS: A business model defines how you create, deliver, and capture value. Economic moats (Buffett) protect profits — network effects, switching costs, cost advantages, intangible assets (brand, patents), and efficient scale. The durability of advantage matters more than its size today.

EXECUTION & STRATEGIC PLANNING: Strategy without execution is hallucination. Align structure, incentives, capabilities, and culture to the strategy. Use OKRs (Objectives and Key Results) to cascade goals. Balanced Scorecard tracks financial, customer, process, and learning metrics. Revisit strategy as conditions change — it's a hypothesis to test, not a monument.

MASTERY LEVELS: L1 — understand competitive forces and positioning. L2-3 — apply strategic frameworks. L4-5 — design competitive strategy and business models. L6-7 — lead strategic transformation. L8 Grandmaster — teach strategy and shape industry-level moves.
