NEGOTIATION — COMPLETE CURRICULUM

FOUNDATIONS: Negotiation is the process of reaching agreement between parties with differing interests. Core principle: negotiation is not about winning against the other side — it's about creating and claiming value. The best outcomes are those where both parties get more than their alternatives (win-win), while you still protect your interests.

BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement): Your BATNA is your walk-away option — what you'll do if no deal is reached. It is your single greatest source of power. Know yours, improve it before negotiating, and estimate the other side's. Never accept terms worse than your BATNA. A strong BATNA lets you negotiate from confidence, not desperation.

ZOPA (Zone of Possible Agreement): The range between your reservation price (worst acceptable) and theirs. If your maximum exceeds their minimum, a deal is possible within that overlap. No ZOPA = no deal unless interests are reframed. The goal is to claim as much of the ZOPA as possible while keeping the deal alive.

INTERESTS vs POSITIONS (Getting to Yes, Fisher & Ury): A position is what someone says they want ("$50,000"); an interest is why they want it (security, status, budget constraint). Positions conflict; interests often don't. Ask "why" and "what for" to uncover interests, then find creative solutions satisfying both sides' underlying needs. Separate the people from the problem.

ANCHORING & FIRST OFFERS: The first number sets the reference point and pulls the final outcome toward it. When you have information, anchor first and ambitiously (but justifiably). When uncertain, let them anchor and reframe. Counter an extreme anchor by re-anchoring, not meeting in the middle. Always justify your number with rationale.

TACTICS & TECHNIQUES: Mirroring (repeat their last words to draw out more). Labeling ("It seems like you're concerned about..."). Calibrated questions ("How am I supposed to do that?" — Chris Voss). The flinch (visible reaction to an offer). Silence (pressure without words). Nibbling (small asks after agreement). Bracketing (offer/counter to converge on target). Trading concessions (never give without getting).

TACTICAL EMPATHY (Chris Voss, Never Split the Difference): Understand and vocalize the other side's perspective and emotions to build trust and gather information. "No" is the start of negotiation, not the end. "That's right" signals genuine agreement (vs "you're right" which ends conversation). Accusation audits — name their likely objections first to defuse them.

MULTI-PARTY & DISTRIBUTIVE vs INTEGRATIVE: Distributive (fixed-pie, claiming value — one wins what the other loses). Integrative (expanding the pie — trading across differently-valued issues to create joint gains). Most real negotiations mix both. In multi-party deals, manage coalitions, sequence conversations, and identify the true decision-makers.

EMOTIONAL CONTROL & LEVERAGE: Leverage is the perception of who needs the deal more. Emotional discipline wins — never negotiate angry or desperate. Time pressure, information asymmetry, and alternatives are leverage sources. Deadlines (real or perceived) drive concessions, usually at the last moment.

MASTERY LEVELS: L1 — understand BATNA, ZOPA, interests. L2-3 — apply anchoring and concession tactics. L4-5 — integrative deal-making and tactical empathy. L6-7 — complex multi-party and high-stakes negotiations. L8 Grandmaster — teach negotiation and design deal architectures.
