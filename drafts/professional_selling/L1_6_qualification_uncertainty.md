# Qualification with Documented Uncertainty

**Level:** 1. **Scope:** Additive instructional draft with hypothetical cases; not a certified completed lesson.

## Outcomes
Evaluate fit, authority, constraints, timing and evidence; separate disqualification from rejection; avoid presenting probability scores as facts.

## Instruction
Qualification asks whether the seller can responsibly help and whether a purchasing process is feasible. It is not a technique for forcing a prospect into a predetermined stage. Record verified information, buyer statements, hypotheses and unknowns separately.

A simple qualification record contains: problem and evidence, desired outcome, product fit, integration requirements, privacy/security conditions, budget process, decision stakeholders, timing and consent status. A “high intent” label based on website visits does not prove buying authority or willingness to pay.

**Worked case:** A team needs offline operation and a vendor's tool requires a live connection. Even with sufficient budget and interest, this is a critical fit gap. The seller should not promise an offline feature that is not implemented. The next step could be to decline the sale, offer a clearly labeled future roadmap discussion or propose a genuinely suitable alternative.

**Risk-adjusted pipeline:** A hypothetical pipeline contains three opportunities: $5,000, $8,000 and $12,000. Illustrative stage probabilities are 20%, 40% and 50%. Weighted value = 5,000 × .2 + 8,000 × .4 + 12,000 × .5 = **$10,200**. This is a planning estimate, not revenue, cash, a guarantee or a validated forecast. Probabilities require calibration against historical outcomes before they should be relied upon.

## Exercises and solutions
1. A buyer likes a demo but security requirements are unknown. Is the opportunity fully qualified? **Answer:** No.
2. A $10,000 opportunity has an illustrative 30% close probability. **Answer:** Weighted estimate $3,000, not booked revenue.
3. A product lacks a required integration. What is the ethical response? **Answer:** Disclose the gap and avoid a promise that it already works.
4. Name two unknowns that should not be guessed. **Answer:** Decision authority and budget approval, for example.

## Mastery
Prepare a qualification record labeling at least three facts, three unknowns and one possible disqualifier. Transfer: qualify a fictional enterprise training purchase without inventing approvals.
