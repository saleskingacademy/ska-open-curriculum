# Objections as Unresolved Information

**Level:** 1. **Scope:** Additive instructional draft with hypothetical cases; not a certified completed lesson.

## Outcomes
Interpret objections as information, distinguish fit gaps from misunderstandings and respond without coercion or fabricated evidence.

## Instruction
An objection may express uncertainty, cost pressure, implementation risk, organizational timing or a genuine mismatch. The seller's job is to clarify, not automatically overcome. A useful sequence is acknowledge, ask a neutral question, test understanding, provide verifiable evidence and allow the buyer to decide.

**Price objection:** “It's too expensive” might mean the price exceeds available funds or that the benefits are unproven. Ask “Which part of the total cost is most difficult to justify?” rather than inventing a discount deadline. A price reduction can worsen unit economics and may not solve a technical mismatch.

**Security objection:** “We cannot share customer records with your tool” is a legitimate constraint. The seller should supply actual data-flow documentation, available controls and unresolved limitations. If requirements cannot be met, do not proceed.

**Worked scenario:** A buyer says the proposed system will save ten hours monthly, but the vendor's demonstration supports only three hours. At an illustrative $40 per hour, three hours corresponds to $120 gross time value monthly. A $250 monthly fee is not justified by that measured time value alone. The seller may explore other measurable outcomes, but cannot convert hypothetical benefits into verified ROI.

**Response example:** Buyer: “Your tool may create mistakes.” Seller: “That's a valid concern. Which errors would be most costly? We can define a supervised test and stop conditions, or conclude it isn't suitable.” This response creates a decision path rather than pressure.

## Exercises and solutions
1. A buyer says implementation would violate policy. **Answer:** Verify the policy requirement and do not recommend an unauthorized deployment.
2. Why is “Everyone else uses it” weak evidence? **Answer:** It neither verifies the claim nor establishes suitability.
3. At $50/hour, what is the gross value of 2.5 hours saved? **Answer:** $125, before costs and uncertainty.
4. Write a respectful response to “Not interested.” **Answer:** Acknowledge the decision, stop unwanted outreach and update suppression where applicable.

## Mastery
Roleplay three objections; distinguish uncertainty, disqualification and opt-out. A response that ignores an explicit refusal fails. Transfer: apply the framework to a buyer concerned about AI-generated educational errors.
