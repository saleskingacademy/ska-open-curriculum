# Cash Timing and Working Capital

**Level:** 1. **Scope:** Additive instructional draft with hypothetical cases; not a certified completed lesson.

## Outcomes
Prepare a cash timeline, calculate a basic cash balance, explain working capital and diagnose a cash shortfall even when sales are growing.

## Instruction
Cash flow measures when money actually enters and leaves an organization. A business may sell successfully but be unable to pay wages because customers pay later than suppliers must be paid. Working capital commonly means current assets minus current liabilities. Its interpretation depends on the business model; a negative figure can signal stress or reflect a prepaid operating model with deferred obligations.

**Worked case:** A company begins the month with $2,500 cash. It collects $4,000 from customers and pays $2,200 in payroll, $1,100 to suppliers and $800 in rent. Ending cash = 2,500 + 4,000 − 2,200 − 1,100 − 800 = $2,400. If a $3,000 supplier payment falls due before customer collections, the firm may experience a temporary deficit even though its month-end cash is positive. Sequence matters.

**Receivables example:** A company invoices $10,000 in a month but collects only $6,000. Assuming no opening receivables, write-offs or adjustments, ending receivables are $4,000. The $10,000 invoiced cannot be substituted for cash collected in a liquidity forecast.

**Scenario analysis:** Forecast base, delayed-collection and higher-expense scenarios. Identify minimum cash, not only ending cash. Keep a documented contingency plan for delays and refunds. For prepaid digital credits, maintain a clear reconciliation between purchases, consumption, refunds and any remaining customer obligations; accounting treatment requires a qualified review.

## Exercises and solutions
1. Opening cash $1,200, receipts $2,800, payments $3,100. **Answer:** $900 closing cash.
2. Current assets $18,000, current liabilities $13,500. **Answer:** $4,500 working capital.
3. Why is a large advance payment not necessarily free cash to spend? **Answer:** The business may still owe service or refunds.
4. What metric would reveal a shortfall on day five despite positive month-end cash? **Answer:** A dated daily or weekly cash forecast showing minimum projected balance.

## Mastery
Produce a dated cash schedule and identify the lowest projected balance, with one adverse scenario and a documented response. Transfer: model a seasonal business with late-paying customers.
