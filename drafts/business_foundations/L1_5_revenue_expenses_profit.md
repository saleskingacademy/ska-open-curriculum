# Revenue, Expenses and Profit

**Level:** 1. **Scope:** Additive instructional draft with hypothetical cases; not a certified completed lesson.

## Outcomes
Distinguish revenue, expenses, profit, cash and gross margin; calculate simple operating results; identify omitted costs and accounting assumptions.

## Instruction
Revenue is the value of goods or services recognized as delivered under the relevant accounting basis. Expenses are costs recognized during the reporting period. Profit is the residual after expenses, but different profit measures include different cost categories. Gross profit equals revenue less cost of goods sold or cost of services. Operating profit also reflects operating expenses such as rent, administrative wages and marketing. Net income may additionally include interest and taxes. Cash receipts and payments can occur in different periods from recognition.

**Worked case:** A hypothetical course provider earns $12,000 in tuition revenue. Direct instructional labor is $3,000 and course materials are $1,000. Gross profit = $8,000; gross margin = 8,000/12,000 = 66.7%. Marketing costs $1,500, administration $2,000 and software $500. Operating profit is $4,000. These figures exclude any additional costs not specified and do not establish actual SKA performance.

**Accrual distinction:** A $1,200 annual subscription paid at the beginning of a contract may create $1,200 cash and an initial service obligation. If performance is satisfied evenly, monthly recognized revenue is $100. Treating the whole advance as earned revenue would overstate delivery in the first month.

**Cost behavior:** Fixed costs do not vary directly with each unit over a relevant range; variable costs do. A per-user processing fee is often variable, while a base software subscription may be fixed within a usage tier. In practice costs can be mixed or stepwise. Use invoices and contracts rather than assuming labels.

## Exercises and solutions
1. Revenue $9,000; direct costs $3,600; operating expenses $2,100. Find gross and operating profit. **Solution:** $5,400 gross; $3,300 operating.
2. A firm collects $600 for three months of equal service. How much is recognized after month one under the stated accrual assumption? **Solution:** $200; remaining obligation $400.
3. Why does positive profit not guarantee adequate cash? **Solution:** Receivables may remain unpaid while bills come due.
4. A seller claims 90% margin but ignores payment fees. What is wrong? **Solution:** The claim omits relevant costs and may use an undefined margin measure.

## Mastery
Reconstruct a basic income statement, identify its reporting basis and explain at least one difference between cash and profit. Transfer: apply the same analysis to a fictional software subscription. No tax or accounting certification is implied.
