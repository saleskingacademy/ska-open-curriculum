# Designing an Offer and Value Proposition

**Level:** 1. **Type:** Additive course expansion; hypothetical cases, not verified SKA operational results.

## Instruction, worked examples and assessment

A value proposition describes a target customer's problem, the improvement offered, the evidence for that improvement and the boundaries of the offer. It is not a slogan or a guarantee. A useful template is: “For [defined customer] experiencing [observed problem], our [specific offering] helps [measurable outcome] by [mechanism], subject to [constraints].” Test each field against evidence. The target customer should be specific enough to evaluate, but not defined using stereotypes.

Distinguish features, benefits and outcomes. A feature is a capability, such as a scheduled report. A benefit is what that capability makes easier, such as reducing manual compilation. An outcome is a measured change, such as reducing median report preparation time from 45 to 20 minutes. A feature does not automatically establish a benefit, and a plausible benefit does not prove an outcome.

**Worked example:** A fictional service provides an automated weekly sales report. A manager currently spends 50 minutes preparing it. A pilot measures 18 minutes with automation, but requires 7 minutes of verification. Net observed time is 25 minutes; saving is 25 minutes weekly, not 32. Over four weeks the measured saving is 100 minutes. A claim of “half the time” is plausible in this sample; a claim of “double your sales” is unsupported. Record the sample period, exceptional cases, errors and excluded setup time.

**Offer boundaries:** State deliverables, timing, customer responsibilities, limitations, support, data-handling expectations, pricing and refund conditions. For subscription software, distinguish onboarding from recurring service. For prepaid credits, describe when credits are purchased, when they are consumed and what happens on failed operations; do not equate credits minted with revenue.

**Exercise 1:** Rewrite “Our system makes every business profitable” into a falsifiable statement. **Solution:** “We will evaluate whether this workflow reduces manual reporting time for a specified group over four weeks, using logged start and finish times.”

**Exercise 2:** A report takes 60 minutes manually, 15 minutes to generate and 10 minutes to verify. Find time saved. **Solution:** 60 − (15 + 10) = 35 minutes, or 58.3% of baseline.

**Exercise 3:** Identify missing terms in “Pay today and our AI does everything.” **Solution:** Defined scope, deliverables, constraints, price, payment terms, limitations, human review and support.

**Mastery gate:** Produce an offer with a defined customer, observed problem, verifiable mechanism, bounded outcome and transparent terms. Reject unqualified guarantees. **Transfer:** Repeat for an education course rather than an automation service.
