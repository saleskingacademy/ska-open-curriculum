# Military Tactics

In the context of military defense, tactics refer to the art and science of employing military forces to achieve a specific objective, such as defeating an enemy or securing a location. A practitioner of military tactics must understand core definitions, first principles, and key vocabulary. 
**Strategy** refers to the overall plan or approach to achieve a long-term goal, such as winning a war or maintaining national security. 
**Operations** involve the execution of strategic plans, encompassing the deployment and employment of military forces. 
**Tactics**, a subset of operations, focus on the specific actions and maneuvers used to achieve a localized objective, such as securing a hill or defeating an enemy unit. 
Key terms include **initiative**, the ability to dictate the pace and nature of a battle; **maneuver**, the movement of forces to gain a strategic or tactical advantage; and **decisive point**, a location or action that offers a significant advantage in achieving the objective. 
Understanding **center of gravity**, the source of an enemy's strength or will to fight, is crucial in devising effective tactics. 
**Principles of war**, such as **mass**, **economy of forces**, **surprise**, and **unity of command**, serve as guidelines for effective tactical decision-making. 
A thorough grasp of these foundational concepts and vocabulary is essential for a practitioner of military tactics to develop and execute effective plans in the pursuit of military objectives. **Command and control (C2)** refers to the exercise of authority and direction over forces, while **logistics** encompasses the planning and execution of supply chain management, maintenance, and transportation. **Principles of war**, such as the objective, initiative, unity of command, and economy of forces, serve as guidelines for tactical planning and execution. A deep understanding of these foundations enables military practitioners to develop and execute effective tactics in various operational environments.

## Mechanisms

In military defense, tactics are the blueprints for executing a mission, and their mechanisms involve a series of coordinated steps. The process begins with Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance (ISR), which gathers information on the enemy's disposition, strength, and movements. This information is then analyzed to identify patterns, vulnerabilities, and potential targets. The next step is Planning, where commanders and staff use the analyzed intelligence to develop a course of action, selecting the most effective tactics and allocating resources accordingly.

The planning phase is followed by Preparation, which involves positioning troops, equipment, and supplies in accordance with the plan. This includes the deployment of forces, the establishment of communication networks, and the implementation of security measures to protect friendly forces. Execution is the next phase, where the planned tactics are put into action, involving the coordination of different units and assets, such as infantry, armor, artillery, and air support.

Throughout the execution phase, commanders continuously assess the situation, making adjustments as necessary to respond to changing circumstances or unexpected enemy actions. This adaptability is crucial, as the fluid nature of combat often necessitates deviations from the original plan. The final step is Evaluation, where the outcome of the tactical operation is assessed, lessons are learned, and these insights are used to refine future tactics. This continuous cycle of ISR, planning, preparation, execution, and evaluation forms the core mechanism of military tactics, allowing forces to adapt, innovate, and ultimately achieve their objectives.

The implementation of military tactics involves a series of coordinated steps, forming a causal chain that ultimately aims to achieve strategic objectives. First, intelligence gathering occurs, where data on the enemy's disposition, strength, and movements is collected through various means such as reconnaissance, surveillance, and human intelligence. Based on this analysis, commanders develop a plan, outlining specific objectives, assigning tasks to different units, and allocating resources. The plan is then communicated to the relevant units through a chain of command, ensuring that each element understands its role and the overall mission objective. Once the plan is set in motion, units execute their assigned tasks, which may involve maneuvers such as flanking, envelopment, or frontal assaults, depending on the tactical situation. As the operation unfolds, real-time intelligence is used to adjust the plan, exploiting successes and mitigating failures. The outcome of these tactical actions contributes to the broader strategic goals, such as securing territory, disrupting enemy supply lines, or weakening the enemy's will to fight. Throughout this process, adaptability and flexibility are crucial, as military tactics must respond to the dynamic nature of combat, where unforeseen circumstances and enemy counteractions can significantly impact the outcome. Effective tactical execution relies on clear communication, disciplined unit cohesion, and the ability to make swift, informed decisions under pressure.

## Methods And Frameworks

In military defense, various methods and frameworks are employed to analyze and respond to threats. The OODA Loop (Observe, Orient, Decide, Act) is a decision-making model used to rapidly respond to changing situations, applicable in dynamic environments where adaptability is crucial. The Military Decision-Making Process (MDMP) is a framework used for deliberate planning, involving steps such as receipt of mission, situation analysis, and course of action development, suitable for complex operations requiring detailed planning. The CARVER matrix is a tool used for target analysis and vulnerability assessment, evaluating factors such as criticality, accessibility, recognizability, vulnerability, effect, and recoverability, useful in identifying and prioritizing targets. The DIME framework (Diplomatic, Informational, Military, Economic) is used to analyze and respond to threats, considering the various instruments of national power, applicable in comprehensive approaches to security. Failure modes for these methods include information overload in the OODA Loop, analysis paralysis in MDMP, and oversimplification of complex systems in CARVER and DIME. Effective application of these methods and frameworks requires a deep understanding of their strengths, limitations, and the context in which they are used. Its failure mode occurs when the loop is not continuously iterated, leading to outdated decisions. The Military Decision-Making Process (MDMP) is a structured framework for planning operations, suitable for complex, large-scale missions. It fails when not tailored to the specific situation or when its iterative nature is not respected. The CARVER matrix (Criticality, Accessibility, Recuperability, Vulnerability, Effect, and Recognizability) is used for target analysis and prioritization, effective when evaluating multiple targets with varying characteristics. Its failure mode is when the criteria are not accurately weighted or when the analysis is not regularly updated. The Principles of War, such as the economy of forces, unity of command, and surprise, serve as guiding principles for military operations, applicable in all contexts but failing when not adapted to the specific operational environment. The CARVER matrix (Criticality, Accessibility, Recuperability, Vulnerability, Effect, and Recognizability) is a framework for assessing the vulnerability of targets, useful for identifying potential weaknesses in enemy defenses. The DIME model (Diplomatic, Informational, Military, Economic) is a framework for understanding the various instruments of national power, applicable when coordinating interagency efforts. The Six Functions of Combat Power (command and control, intelligence, maneuver, firepower, protection, and sustainment) provide a structured approach to analyzing and planning military operations, suitable for large-scale campaigns. Failure modes for these methods include information overload in the OODA Loop, incorrect weighting of factors in the CARVER matrix, and inadequate coordination between different instruments of national power in the DIME model. Understanding these methods and frameworks is crucial for effective military planning and execution.

## Common Errors

In military tactics, common errors often stem from a lack of understanding of fundamental principles or a failure to adapt to changing situations. One such error is the failure to maintain security, particularly in the form of inadequate reconnaissance and poor situational awareness. This can lead to being caught off guard by enemy forces, resulting in unnecessary casualties and loss of initiative. Another mistake is the underestimation or overestimation of enemy capabilities, leading to inadequate planning and resource allocation. Additionally, the failure to prioritize clear and concise communication can result in confusion and misexecution of orders, compromising the success of an operation. The principle of mass, which involves concentrating forces at the decisive point, is also often misunderstood or misapplied, leading to a dispersion of effort and a lack of decisive action. Furthermore, the failure to consider the terrain and its impact on military operations can lead to costly mistakes, such as funneling troops into kill zones or neglecting to secure key terrain features. These errors can be attributed to a lack of training, inexperience, or a failure to learn from past mistakes, highlighting the importance of ongoing education and self-reflection in the development of effective military tactics. This can result from poor intelligence gathering or a failure to consider alternative perspectives. Another error is the failure to maintain security and secrecy, allowing the enemy to gain valuable information about friendly forces' dispositions and intentions. Overreliance on technology is also a common mistake, as it can lead to a lack of basic fieldcraft skills and an inability to adapt when technology fails. Additionally, poor communication and coordination between units can lead to confusion, delays, and a lack of effective command and control. The principle of economy of forces is also often neglected, where available forces are not concentrated at the decisive point, leading to a lack of sufficient mass to achieve the desired outcome. These errors can be mitigated by thorough planning, effective intelligence gathering, and a focus on developing well-rounded tactical skills.

## Advanced

At the graduate level, military tactics delve into complex, nuanced concepts that integrate multiple disciplines, including operational art, strategic studies, and advanced technologies. One key area of focus is the study of nonlinear warfare, where the traditional distinctions between combatant and non-combatant, as well as the notion of a clear "front line," become increasingly blurred. This requires an understanding of network-centric warfare, where the flow of information and the ability to disrupt or dominate networks become critical components of tactical planning.

The incorporation of artificial intelligence (AI), autonomous systems, and cyber warfare into tactical operations presents both opportunities and challenges. AI can enhance situational awareness, accelerate decision-making, and optimize resource allocation, but it also raises questions about accountability, ethical decision-making, and the potential for unforeseen consequences. The integration of autonomous systems, such as drones and unmanned ground vehicles, into tactical formations is another area of active research, with implications for force structure, logistics, and command and control.

Open questions in the field include how to effectively counter anti-access/area denial (A2/AD) strategies, which use advanced missile systems and other technologies to deny an adversary access to a theater of operation. The development of effective countermeasures against such strategies is a pressing concern for military planners. Additionally, the role of information operations and psychological warfare in shaping the tactical environment is an area of ongoing study, as is the impact of urbanization on military tactics, given that future conflicts are increasingly likely to occur in densely populated urban areas.

The field is moving towards a more integrated understanding of the physical and informational dimensions of warfare, recognizing that tactical success depends on the ability to dominate both the physical battlefield and the flow of information. This requires a deep understanding of the interplay between technology, doctrine, and organizational culture, as well as the ability to adapt rapidly to changing circumstances. As such, graduate-level study in military tactics emphasizes the development of critical thinking, problem-solving, and leadership skills, preparing officers and defense professionals to navigate the complexities of modern warfare.

Another area of advanced study is the incorporation of emerging technologies, such as artificial intelligence (AI), cyber warfare, and hypersonic systems, into tactical doctrine. This involves examining how these technologies can be leveraged to enhance situational awareness, accelerate decision-making, and increase the speed and precision of military operations. However, it also raises important questions about the potential risks and unintended consequences of relying on these technologies, including issues related to command and control, accountability, and the potential for escalation.

The field is also moving towards a greater emphasis on interdisciplinary approaches, incorporating insights from social sciences, psychology, and anthropology to better understand the human and cultural dimensions of conflict. This includes the study of irregular warfare, where tactics must be adapted to address the complexities of counterinsurgency, counterterrorism, and stability operations. Furthermore, there is a growing recognition of the importance of "whole of government" approaches, which require military tacticians to work closely with civilian agencies, non-governmental organizations, and local partners to achieve strategic objectives. Ultimately, the advanced study of military tactics requires a deep understanding of the complex, dynamic nature of modern conflict and the ability to adapt and innovate in response to evolving challenges and opportunities.
