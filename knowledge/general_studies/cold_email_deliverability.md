# Cold Email Deliverability

> The course teaches applied practice of cold email deliverability, assuming foundational knowledge of email marketing and related concepts.

## Overview

Professional discipline for the prospect specialist: cold email deliverability. Applied practice used to generate revenue.

## Foundations

Cold email deliverability refers to the ability of an email to reach the recipient's inbox, rather than being blocked or filtered as spam. A practitioner must understand key concepts: **Email Service Providers (ESPs)**, such as Gmail or Outlook, which manage email accounts and filter incoming emails. **Internet Protocol (IP) addresses** are unique numerical identifiers assigned to devices, used to route emails. **Domain Names** are human-readable identifiers, such as example.com, which are mapped to IP addresses. **Sender Policy Framework (SPF)** is a protocol that verifies the IP address of the sender, ensuring it is authorized to send emails on behalf of the domain. **Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance (DMARC)** is a protocol that verifies the authenticity of emails, preventing spoofing and phishing attacks. Understanding these concepts is crucial for optimizing cold email deliverability.

## Mechanisms

The process of cold email deliverability involves a series of steps that determine whether an email reaches the recipient's inbox. It begins with the sender's email service provider (ESP) or mail transfer agent (MTA) forwarding the email to the recipient's ESP or MTA. The recipient's ESP then checks the sender's IP address against its blacklist to determine if it has a history of sending spam. If the IP address is not blacklisted, the email is then scanned for spam keywords and phrases using Bayesian filters or other machine learning algorithms. The email's sender policy framework (SPF) and domain-based message authentication, reporting, and conformance (DMARC) records are also verified to ensure the email is coming from a legitimate source. If these checks pass, the email is then routed to the recipient's inbox, where it may still be subject to additional filtering by the recipient's email client. The causal chain is as follows: IP address reputation → blacklist check → content scanning → authentication checks (SPF and DMARC) → routing to inbox → final filtering by email client. Understanding these mechanisms is crucial for optimizing cold email deliverability.

## Methods And Frameworks

The Bounce Rate Method involves tracking the percentage of emails that bounce back, indicating poor deliverability. Use when monitoring email campaigns' initial performance. Failure mode: over-reliance on bounce rates alone, neglecting spam filter evasion. 
The Sender Score Formula calculates a sender's reputation based on factors like complaint rates and unknown user rates. Apply when evaluating sender reputation. Failure mode: misinterpreting the score as the sole indicator of deliverability. 
The CAN-SPAM Compliance Framework ensures adherence to anti-spam laws, enhancing deliverability. Implement when sending commercial emails. Failure mode: incomplete or inaccurate implementation, leading to legal issues. 
The IP Warm-up Method gradually increases email volume from a new IP address to prevent flagging as spam. Use when introducing a new IP address. Failure mode: rushing the warm-up process, triggering spam filters. 
The Content Scoring Model evaluates email content's spam likelihood based on keywords, links, and formatting. Apply when crafting email content. Failure mode: over-reliance on content scoring, neglecting other deliverability factors. 
The Domain Alignment Method involves aligning the "from" domain with the email content and sender's identity. Use when building sender reputation. Failure mode: inconsistent domain alignment, confusing recipients and spam filters. 
The Engagement-Based Method prioritizes email content and sender reputation based on recipient engagement. Apply when optimizing email campaigns. Failure mode: over-emphasizing engagement metrics, neglecting other deliverability aspects.

## Worked Examples

To illustrate the principles of cold email deliverability, consider the following scenarios. 
1. **Domain Warm-up**: A company, XYZ Inc., has a new domain (xyzinc.com) and wants to send 10,000 cold emails per day. Initially, their domain has a low reputation score (10/100). To improve deliverability, they start by sending 100 emails/day for the first week, increasing by 100 emails/day each subsequent week. This gradual increase helps to warm up the domain, reducing the likelihood of being flagged as spam. 
2. **Bounce Rate Management**: An email marketer, ABC, sends 5,000 cold emails with a 5% bounce rate (250 bounces). To maintain a healthy bounce rate (<2%), ABC must remove or correct the bounced email addresses and ensure the email list is up-to-date. By doing so, ABC can reduce the bounce rate to 1.5% (75 bounces) in the next campaign, improving deliverability.
3. **SPF and DKIM Authentication**: A business, DEF, uses a third-party email service provider (ESP) to send cold emails. To authenticate their emails and improve deliverability, DEF sets up SPF (Sender Policy Framework) and DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) records. By doing so, DEF can verify the sender's identity and reduce the risk of spoofing, resulting in a higher deliverability rate (95% vs. 80% without authentication).

## Common Errors

Practitioners often make mistakes that negatively impact cold email deliverability. One common error is using generic or misleading subject lines, which can trigger spam filters. Another mistake is including too many links or attachments, as this can raise red flags with email service providers. Additionally, using all caps or excessive punctuation in the email body can be perceived as spammy. Failure to warm up new email accounts by gradually increasing send volume can also lead to deliverability issues. Furthermore, neglecting to authenticate email domains using SPF, DKIM, and DMARC protocols can result in emails being flagged as unverified or spam. Using purchased or rented email lists, rather than opt-in lists, can also harm deliverability and lead to blacklisting. Lastly, not regularly cleaning and updating email lists can cause bounces and complaints, further damaging deliverability. These errors can be avoided by understanding how spam filters and email service providers work, and by prioritizing best practices for email content and list management.
