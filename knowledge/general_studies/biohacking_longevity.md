## Foundations

Biohacking longevity refers to the practice of using science and technology to optimize and enhance human physiological function, with the goal of promoting healthy aging and increasing lifespan. A practitioner of biohacking longevity must understand key concepts, including **homeostasis**, the ability of the body to maintain a stable internal environment despite changes in external conditions. **Epigenetics**, the study of gene expression and how environmental factors influence genetic traits, also plays a crucial role. **Senescence**, the process of cellular aging, is a fundamental aspect, as is **mitochondrial function**, which refers to the energy-producing structures within cells. **Telomere length**, a measure of the protective caps on chromosome ends, is another important factor, as shorter telomeres are associated with aging. **Oxidative stress**, an imbalance between free radicals and antioxidants, can damage cells and contribute to aging. Understanding these concepts and their interplay is essential for developing effective biohacking strategies to promote longevity. **Biomarkers**, measurable indicators of physiological function, are used to track progress and inform treatment decisions. A strong foundation in these principles is necessary for a practitioner to design and implement personalized biohacking plans.

Another mistake is the failure to monitor and adjust interventions based on biomarker feedback, such as neglecting to track changes in telomere length, epigenetic markers, or inflammatory responses.
