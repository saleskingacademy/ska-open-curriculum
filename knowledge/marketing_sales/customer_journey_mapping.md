# Customer Journey Mapping

> The course teaches applied practice of customer journey mapping, assuming some prior knowledge of marketing and sales concepts.

## Overview

Professional discipline for the funnel specialist: customer journey mapping. Applied practice used to generate revenue.

## Foundations

Customer journey mapping is a visual representation of the stages a customer goes through when interacting with a product, service, or organization, from initial awareness to post-purchase engagement. A **customer** is an individual or organization that purchases or uses a product or service. The **customer journey** encompasses all touchpoints, defined as any point of interaction between the customer and the organization, including **online channels** (e.g., website, social media) and **offline channels** (e.g., physical stores, customer support). **Touchpoint mapping** is the process of identifying and documenting these interactions. A **journey map** is a diagram that illustrates the customer's experience across these touchpoints, highlighting **pain points** (challenges or frustrations) and **moments of truth** (critical interactions that significantly impact the customer's perception). **Personas**, detailed profiles of target customers, are often used to guide the mapping process, ensuring the journey map reflects the needs and behaviors of the organization's ideal customer. **Empathy mapping**, a related technique, involves understanding the customer's thoughts, feelings, and actions at each stage of the journey. By applying these foundational concepts, practitioners can create effective customer journey maps that inform strategic decisions and drive customer-centric design.

## Mechanisms

Customer journey mapping is a structured process that involves several key steps. It begins with defining the scope and objectives of the mapping exercise, including identifying the specific customer segment and journey stage to be mapped. The next step is to gather data on the customer's thoughts, feelings, and actions throughout their journey, typically through research methods such as surveys, interviews, and observations. This data is then used to create a visual representation of the customer's journey, often in the form of a timeline or map, which highlights the various touchpoints and interactions the customer has with the organization. The causal chain is made explicit by identifying the triggers, pain points, and moments of truth that drive the customer's behavior and decision-making at each stage of the journey. By analyzing these factors, organizations can identify opportunities to improve the customer experience, reduce friction, and increase satisfaction, ultimately leading to increased loyalty and retention. The mapping process also involves identifying the key performance indicators (KPIs) and metrics that will be used to measure the effectiveness of any changes made to the customer journey.

## Methods And Frameworks

Customer journey mapping utilizes various methods and frameworks to visualize and analyze the customer experience. The Touchpoint Mapping method is used to identify and map all touchpoints a customer interacts with during their journey, helping to identify pain points and areas for improvement. The Empathy Mapping model is employed to understand customer thoughts, feelings, and behaviors at each stage of the journey, allowing for a more empathetic and customer-centric approach. The Service Blueprint framework is utilized to map the frontstage and backstage processes, highlighting the interactions between the customer, employees, and systems. The Customer Journey Canvas formula is used to visualize the customer journey, identifying the stages, actions, and emotions involved. Each method has its failure mode, such as neglecting to involve stakeholders in the Touchpoint Mapping process, failing to validate empathy maps with real customer data, or not considering the organizational capabilities and resources when using the Service Blueprint. Understanding these methods, models, and formulas, as well as their limitations, is crucial for effective customer journey mapping.

## Worked Examples

A company selling outdoor gear wants to map the customer journey for a new hiking backpack. The journey starts with awareness, where 1000 potential customers see an ad, with 200 visiting the website (20% conversion rate). Of those, 50 request more information (25% conversion rate), and 20 make a purchase (40% conversion rate). The customer journey map reveals a significant drop-off between awareness and consideration, suggesting the need for more engaging website content.

Another example involves a telecom company, where customers report frustration with the billing process. The journey map shows that 80% of customers use the website to pay bills, but 30% encounter errors, leading to a 20% abandonment rate. By streamlining the billing process and improving error handling, the company can reduce customer frustration and increase payment completion rates.

A third example is a retail bank, where the customer journey map for account opening reveals that 60% of customers start the process online, but only 30% complete it. The map highlights a pain point where customers must print and mail paperwork, causing a significant drop-off. By implementing an e-signature process, the bank can increase completion rates and improve the overall customer experience.

To illustrate the application of customer journey mapping, consider the following examples. 
1. A retail bank wants to improve its mortgage application process. The current process has a customer satisfaction rating of 60%. By mapping the customer journey, the bank identifies pain points such as lengthy paperwork and unclear communication. By streamlining the process and implementing a digital platform, the bank reduces the application time by 30% and increases customer satisfaction to 80%. 
2. An e-commerce company notices a high cart abandonment rate of 25%. Through customer journey mapping, it discovers that customers are frustrated with the checkout process, citing too many steps and lack of payment options. The company simplifies the checkout process, reducing the number of steps by 40%, and introduces additional payment methods. As a result, the cart abandonment rate decreases to 15%. 
3. A healthcare provider aims to enhance the patient experience in its outpatient clinics. By mapping the patient journey, it identifies wait times and lack of communication as major pain points. The provider implements a digital check-in system, reducing wait times by 25%, and introduces a patient portal for appointment scheduling and test results. Patient satisfaction increases from 70% to 85%. 
In each example, customer journey mapping helps identify areas for improvement, leading to targeted interventions that enhance the customer experience and drive business outcomes.

## Common Errors

A common error is assuming customer journey mapping is solely a creative exercise, neglecting the need for rigorous data collection and analysis to inform the mapping process. Another mistake is focusing too narrowly on a specific touchpoint or interaction, rather than considering the entire end-to-end customer experience. Practitioners also often fail to involve stakeholders from across the organization, resulting in maps that lack a comprehensive understanding of the customer's interactions with different departments and functions. Furthermore, maps are sometimes created without a clear definition of the customer segment being targeted, leading to generic and ineffective mappings. Additionally, the dynamic nature of customer behavior is often overlooked, with maps being treated as static representations rather than living documents that require regular updating and refinement. By recognizing these common errors, practitioners can take a more informed and effective approach to customer journey mapping.

A common error is assuming customer journey mapping is solely about creating a visual representation, rather than using it as a tool for deeper understanding of customer experiences. This can lead to inaccurate or incomplete journey maps. Furthermore, neglecting to prioritize and address the pain points and opportunities identified during the mapping process can render the exercise ineffective.
