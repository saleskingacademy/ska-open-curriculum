## Overview

Deliberate large-scale intervention in Earth's climate to counter global warming. Two categories: Carbon Dioxide Removal (CDR) and Solar Radiation Management (SRM). CONTROVERSIAL — even discussing it is contentious (moral hazard — could reduce urgency to cut emissions). CDR: Direct Air Capture (DAC — fans pull air through chemical solvents/sorbents that bind CO2. Climeworks, Carbon Engineering. Current cost $400-600/ton, need $100/ton for scale), Enhanced weathering (spread crushed basalt on farmland — accelerates natural CO2 absorption), Ocean alkalinity enhancement (add minerals to ocean to increase CO2 absorption capacity), BECCS (Bioenergy with CCS — grow biomass, burn for energy, capture emissions), Afforestation/reforestation (cheapest, most established, but land-use competition), Biochar (pyrolyzed biomass buried in soil — stable carbon sequestration + soil improvement). SRM: Stratospheric Aerosol Injection (spray sulfate particles in stratosphere to reflect sunlight — mimics volcanic eruption. Pinatubo 1991 cooled Earth 0.5°C. Cheap but: doesn't address ocean acidification, termination shock if stopped, regional precipitation changes, governance nightmare — who controls the thermostat?), Marine Cloud Brightening (spray sea salt to brighten clouds → reflect more sunlight), Space-based sunshades (theoretical — L1 Lagrange point parasols). GOVERNANCE: No international framework for geoengineering. Unilateral action risk. Environmental modification treaty (ENMOD 1976) applies to warfare. Oxford Principles (2011) — regulate as public good, independent assessment, public participation, open publication, governance before deployment. CARBON MARKETS: Voluntary ($2B) and compliance (EU ETS, $900B). Carbon credits, offsets. Quality concerns (Verra controversy). APPLIED: Paris Agreement targets (1.5°C) likely require some CDR even with aggressive emission cuts. Understanding this space is essential for climate-conscious business strategy.

## Foundations

## Mechanisms

Climate engineering, also known as geoengineering, involves large-scale technological interventions in the Earth's climate system to counteract the effects of global warming. The mechanisms of climate engineering can be broadly categorized into two main approaches: carbon dioxide removal (CDR) and solar radiation management (SRM). CDR methods aim to reduce the amount of CO2 in the atmosphere, thereby decreasing the greenhouse effect. This can be achieved through afforestation/reforestation, soil carbon sequestration, and ocean fertilization, which enhance the natural carbon sinks. SRM methods, on the other hand, involve reflecting a small percentage of the sun's radiation back into space, thereby reducing the amount of solar energy absorbed by the Earth. This can be achieved through stratospheric aerosol injection, cloud brightening, and space-based reflectors. The causal chain of climate engineering involves the following steps: 
1. Identification of the climate problem: Increased CO2 levels and global temperatures.
2. Selection of the geoengineering method: CDR or SRM, depending on the desired outcome.
3. Implementation of the method: Deployment of the chosen technology, such as afforestation or stratospheric aerosol injection.
4. Alteration of the climate system: Removal of CO2 or reduction of solar radiation, leading to a decrease in global temperatures.
5. Feedback mechanisms: The climate system responds to the changes, potentially triggering feedback loops, such as changes in ocean currents or ice sheet melting.
6. Monitoring and evaluation: Continuous assessment of the effectiveness and potential side effects of the geoengineering method, allowing for adjustments and improvements. For SRM, the causal chain involves: (1) injection or deployment of reflective materials, (2) increase in Earth's albedo, (3) reduction in solar radiation absorption, and (4) cooling of the planet. ## Methods And Frameworks

In the context of climate engineering within sustainability environment, several methods and frameworks are employed to assess and mitigate the impacts of climate change. The Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) framework is used to evaluate the economic viability of climate engineering projects, weighing the costs of implementation against the potential benefits of reduced climate-related damages. The Integrated Assessment Models (IAMs) are utilized to simulate the complex interactions between climate, economy, and society, providing a comprehensive understanding of the potential consequences of climate engineering interventions. The Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) method is applied to evaluate the environmental impacts of climate engineering technologies, from production to end-of-life, helping to identify potential failure modes and areas for improvement. The Precautionary Principle is a guiding framework that emphasizes caution and careful consideration when implementing climate engineering solutions, acknowledging the potential for unintended consequences and irreversible damage. Failure modes of these methods include overreliance on economic valuation, neglecting social and environmental externalities, and underestimating the complexity and uncertainty of climate systems. The Adaptive Management (AM) approach is used to address these limitations, promoting iterative learning, flexibility, and stakeholder engagement in the development and implementation of climate engineering strategies.

Reasoning: Calculate the amount of CO2 captured (1,000,000 tons * 0.9) = 900,000 tons. Multiply by the capture cost ($50/ton * 900,000 tons) = $45,000,000 per year.
2. **Solar Radiation Management (SRM)**: A proposal suggests injecting 1 million tons of sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere to reflect 1% of incoming solar radiation. If the injection cost is $10 per ton, what is the total cost of SRM? 
Reasoning: Calculate the total cost by multiplying the amount of sulfur dioxide injected (1,000,000 tons) by the injection cost ($10/ton) = $10,000,000.
3. **Afforestation/Reforestation**: A project aims to plant 100,000 hectares of forest to sequester carbon dioxide. If the planting cost is $500 per hectare and each hectare sequesters 200 tons of CO2 over 50 years, what is the cost per ton of CO2 sequestered? 
Reasoning: Calculate the total planting cost (100,000 hectares * $500/hectare) = $50,000,000. Calculate the total CO2 sequestered (100,000 hectares * 200 tons/hectare) = 20,000,000 tons. Calculate the cost per ton ($50,000,000 / 20,000,000 tons) = $2.50 per ton.

## Common Errors

In the field of climate engineering within sustainability environments, practitioners often make critical mistakes that can have significant consequences. One common error is the assumption that climate engineering techniques can be used as a sole solution to mitigate climate change, rather than as a complement to reduction of greenhouse gas emissions. This oversight neglects the complexity of the climate system and the potential for unintended consequences, such as disrupting global precipitation patterns or exacerbating ocean acidification. Another mistake is the lack of consideration for the long-term effects of climate engineering interventions, such as the potential for dependence on continuous deployment of climate engineering technologies, which could lead to a loss of natural climate resilience. Furthermore, some practitioners fail to account for the social and economic implications of climate engineering, including issues of equity, justice, and governance, which can lead to unequal distribution of benefits and risks. Additionally, the use of climate models that do not accurately represent the complexities of the climate system can lead to flawed decision-making and ineffective climate engineering strategies. It is essential for practitioners to adopt a holistic and precautionary approach, considering the potential risks and benefits of climate engineering techniques and prioritizing reduction of greenhouse gas emissions as the primary strategy for mitigating climate change.
