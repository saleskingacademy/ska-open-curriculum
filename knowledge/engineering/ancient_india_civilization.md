## Indus Valley (3300-1300 Bce)

Harappa, Mohenjo-daro. Advanced urban planning — grid streets, indoor plumbing, standardized weights, granaries. Population ~5M. Writing (undeciphered Indus script — 400+ symbols). Declined ~1900 BCE — climate change, river shifts. VEDIC PERIOD (1500-500 BCE): Indo-Aryan migration. Rigveda (~1500 BCE — oldest Hindu scripture, 1028 hymns). Caste system origins. Sanskrit literature. Upanishads (~800-500 BCE — philosophical texts, Brahman-Atman, karma, moksha, samsara). MAURYA EMPIRE (322-185 BCE): Chandragupta Maurya. Ashoka (268-232 BCE — converted to Buddhism after Kalinga War, Rock Edicts, promoted dharma across empire, first known ruler to advocate religious tolerance and animal welfare). GUPTA EMPIRE (320-550 CE): Golden Age. MATHEMATICS: Zero as a number (Brahmagupta 628 CE), decimal place-value system (what we call "Arabic" numerals — actually Indian), negative numbers, quadratic formula, π calculated to 4 decimal places (Aryabhata). ASTRONOMY: Aryabhata (476 CE) — Earth rotates on axis, heliocentric ideas, eclipses are shadows. MEDICINE: Sushruta Samhita (600 BCE) — 300+ surgical procedures, rhinoplasty (nose reconstruction), 120+ surgical instruments. Ayurveda system. LITERATURE: Mahabharata (100K+ verses, longest epic ever written, contains Bhagavad Gita), Ramayana. Kalidasa (Shakespeare of India). Grammar: Panini (4th century BCE — Ashtadhyayi, 3959 rules, considered most sophisticated grammar ever written, influenced modern computational linguistics).

This system influenced the distribution of resources, with each varna having distinct roles and responsibilities. The caste system, which emerged later, further solidified these divisions, affecting social mobility and interaction. 
Economically, ancient India was primarily agrarian, with the majority of the population engaged in agriculture. The system of taxation, where a portion of the agricultural produce was given to the rulers, supported the administrative and military apparatus. Trade and commerce also played significant roles, with the establishment of trade routes and the use of coins facilitating economic transactions. 
Politically, ancient India saw the rise and fall of various kingdoms and empires, such as the Mauryan and Gupta empires, which implemented administrative systems, including the division of their territories into smaller units for easier governance. The concept of dharma (duty) was central to the functioning of these societies, guiding individual and collective behavior. 
The educational mechanism was primarily based on the guru-shishya parampara (teacher-disciple tradition), where knowledge was passed down from the guru to the shishya. This tradition played a crucial role in preserving and transmitting ancient Indian knowledge, including the Vedas and other religious texts. 
These mechanisms interacted and influenced one another, creating a complex and dynamic societal structure that underpinned the ancient Indian civilization.
