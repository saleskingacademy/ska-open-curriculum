## Foundations

International business refers to the exchange of goods, services, and resources across national borders, involving firms, governments, and individuals. A key concept is globalization, defined as the increasing economic interdependence among nations, characterized by the free flow of goods, services, and capital. Multinational enterprises (MNEs) are firms that operate in multiple countries, managing and controlling resources and activities beyond their home country. Foreign direct investment (FDI) occurs when a firm invests in a foreign country, establishing or acquiring a business, and exercising control over it. Exporting and importing are fundamental activities, where exporting involves selling goods or services to another country, and importing involves buying goods or services from another country. International trade theories, such as comparative advantage and absolute advantage, explain why countries engage in international trade. Comparative advantage, introduced by David Ricardo, states that countries should specialize in producing goods for which they have a lower opportunity cost, while absolute advantage, introduced by Adam Smith, suggests that countries should produce goods for which they have a lower absolute cost. Understanding these concepts and terms is essential for practitioners in international business.

## Mechanisms

In international business, mechanisms refer to the processes and systems that facilitate cross-border transactions and operations. The causal chain begins with market research, where firms identify foreign market opportunities and assess their feasibility. This involves analyzing market size, growth potential, competition, and regulatory environments. Upon identifying a viable market, firms must choose an entry mode, such as exporting, joint ventures, or foreign direct investment (FDI). Exporting involves selling products or services to foreign customers, while joint ventures and FDI require partnering with local firms or establishing a physical presence in the foreign market. The next step is to establish a logistics and supply chain system, which includes transportation, inventory management, and customs clearance. Firms must also navigate foreign regulatory environments, including tax laws, labor laws, and trade regulations. Payment and financing mechanisms, such as letters of credit and export financing, are also crucial in facilitating international transactions. Finally, firms must manage currency exchange risks and cultural differences to ensure successful international operations. Throughout these mechanisms, firms must balance the benefits of international expansion with the risks and challenges associated with operating in foreign markets.

## Methods And Frameworks

In international business, several methods and frameworks are employed to analyze and navigate global markets. The PESTEL framework is used to assess macro-environmental factors, including political, economic, socio-cultural, technological, environmental, and legal aspects, when evaluating market entry or expansion. The SWOT analysis is applied to identify a company's strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats in a global context. The Porter's Diamond model is utilized to examine the competitiveness of nations and industries, considering factors such as factor conditions, demand conditions, related and supporting industries, and firm strategy, structure, and rivalry. The Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions framework is employed to understand cultural differences and their impact on business operations, including power distance, individualism, uncertainty avoidance, and masculinity. Each of these methods has its failure mode, such as overemphasis on a single aspect or failure to consider dynamic changes in the global environment. The Dunning's Eclectic Paradigm is used to explain foreign direct investment, considering ownership, location, and internalization advantages. The Uppsala model is applied to describe the internationalization process of firms, including the stages of no regular export activities, export via independent representatives, and wholly owned subsidiaries. Understanding these methods and frameworks is crucial for businesses to develop effective international strategies and mitigate potential risks.

First, calculate the cost price in GBP: $80 * 0.82 = £65.60. 
Then, determine the selling price in USD with a 20% margin: $80 + (20% of $80) = $80 + $16 = $96. 
Finally, convert the selling price to GBP: $96 * 0.82 = £78.72. 
2. **Tariff Calculation**: A company imports 500 units of a product from China to the US, with a unit cost of $50. The US imposes a 10% tariff on this product. What is the total tariff paid? 
Tariff per unit = $50 * 10% = $5. 
Total tariff = $5 * 500 units = $2,500. 
3. **Foreign Investment**: A company invests $1 million in a subsidiary in Japan, expecting a 5% annual return in local currency (JPY). If the exchange rate is 1 USD = 110 JPY and the annual return in JPY is 5.5 million JPY, what is the return in USD?

## Applications

In international business, companies apply various strategies to expand globally, manage risks, and capitalize on opportunities. Multinational corporations (MNCs) use foreign market entry modes such as exporting, licensing, joint ventures, and wholly-owned subsidiaries to access new markets. Companies like Coca-Cola and McDonald's use a combination of these modes to establish a presence in multiple countries. International business also involves managing global supply chains, logistics, and distribution networks to ensure efficient delivery of products. Firms like Apple and Samsung rely on global sourcing to procure components and materials from low-cost countries. Furthermore, international business involves navigating diverse regulatory environments, tax systems, and cultural differences to ensure compliance and effectiveness. Companies like IBM and Microsoft have established global teams to manage these complexities and leverage local market knowledge to drive growth. Additionally, international business involves managing foreign exchange risks, hedging currency exposures, and optimizing global financial management. Companies like Toyota and Volkswagen use financial instruments like forwards, futures, and options to mitigate currency risks and maintain competitiveness in global markets.

Overall, the application of international business principles enables companies to operate effectively in a globalized economy, capitalize on new market opportunities, and manage the complexities of cross-border business operations.

## Common Errors

In international business, practitioners often make mistakes due to a lack of understanding of cultural, economic, and political differences between countries. One common error is the assumption of homogeneity across markets, where businesses apply the same strategies and practices used in their home market to foreign markets without adaptation. This can lead to misunderstandings and misinterpretations of local consumer needs and preferences. Another error is the underestimation of the impact of institutional factors, such as government regulations, taxation, and labor laws, which can significantly affect business operations and profitability. Furthermore, businesses may overlook the importance of local partnerships and networking, which are crucial for navigating complex foreign markets and establishing trust with local stakeholders. Additionally, the failure to conduct thorough market research and analysis can result in inaccurate assessments of market size, growth potential, and competitive landscape, leading to poor investment decisions. These errors can be attributed to a lack of international business acumen, inadequate training, and insufficient cultural intelligence, highlighting the need for businesses to develop a deeper understanding of the complexities of international business environments. This can lead to failed product launches or marketing campaigns. Additionally, businesses may overlook the importance of adapting their products or services to meet local standards and regulations, leading to non-compliance issues. The failure to conduct thorough market research and analyze local competition can also lead to poor investment decisions. Furthermore, neglecting to consider the impact of exchange rates, inflation, and other economic factors on international trade can result in significant financial losses. These errors can be attributed to a lack of research, inadequate planning, and insufficient cultural intelligence, highlighting the need for businesses to develop a deeper understanding of the international business environment.
