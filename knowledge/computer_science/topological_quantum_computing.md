## Foundations

Topological quantum computing relies on the principles of topology and quantum mechanics. **Topology** is the study of the properties of shapes and spaces that are preserved under continuous deformations, such as stretching and bending. In this context, **topological invariants** are quantities that remain unchanged under these deformations, and they play a crucial role in characterizing the properties of topological systems. **Quantum mechanics** is a fundamental theory that describes the behavior of matter and energy at the smallest scales, where **wave functions** encode the probability of finding a particle in a particular state. A **qubit** (quantum bit) is the fundamental unit of quantum information, which can exist in a **superposition** of states (0 and 1) and become **entangled** with other qubits, meaning their properties are correlated regardless of distance. **Anyons** are exotic quasiparticles that can arise in topological systems, and they are characterized by their **braiding statistics**, which describe how they interact with each other when exchanged. The **Fibonacci anyon** is a specific type of anyon that has been proposed as a basis for topological quantum computing due to its non-abelian statistics, which allow for universal quantum computation. Understanding these concepts is essential for a practitioner of topological quantum computing, as they form the basis for the development of **topological quantum gates** and **quantum algorithms** that can be used to perform robust and fault-tolerant quantum computations.
