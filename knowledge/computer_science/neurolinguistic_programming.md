Neurolinguistic Programming (NLP) is a social science approach that examines the relationship between neurological processes, language, and behavioral patterns. **Neuro** refers to the nervous system and brain functions, **linguistic** pertains to language and its structures, and **programming** denotes the idea that behavior and cognitive processes can be modified and improved. A core principle of NLP is that individuals create their own reality through their perceptions, which are influenced by their internal representations and language use. **Internal representations** are the mental images, sounds, and feelings that people use to make sense of their experiences. NLP practitioners use various techniques, such as **reframing** (changing the perspective or meaning of an experience) and **anchoring** (associating a stimulus with a response), to help individuals modify their internal representations and behaviors. Key vocabulary in NLP includes **meta-model** (a set of questions used to challenge and clarify language patterns), **submodalities** (specific characteristics of internal representations, such as visual, auditory, or kinesthetic), and **rapport** (a state of mutual understanding and connection between individuals). Understanding these core definitions and principles is essential for NLP practitioners to effectively work with clients and facilitate positive change.
