# AI For Crypto Market Analysis

## Overview

AI for Crypto Market Analysis teaches how AI and data analysis are applied to digital-asset markets, what the methods can and cannot tell you, and how to manage risk. This subject is education only and does not give investment advice.

## Level 1-2: Foundations

Market data includes price, trading volume and order books from exchanges. On-chain data comes directly from the blockchain, such as transaction counts, active addresses and flows between wallets.

Sentiment data measures the tone of news and social media about an asset. It is noisy and can be manipulated, so it is weak evidence on its own.

No model can reliably predict prices. Markets react to new information, and crypto markets are especially volatile, with large moves driven by news, liquidity and leverage.

## Level 3-4: How It Works

Machine learning models look for patterns that were associated with price moves in the past. Classification models estimate the chance of an up or down move; regression models estimate the size of a move.

Backtesting tests a strategy on historical data. It is essential but misleading if done carelessly, because a model can be tuned to fit the past perfectly and fail in the future. This is called overfitting.

Language models can summarize news, filings and project documents quickly, but they can misstate facts, so every figure must be checked against the source.

## Level 5-6: Implementation

Separate training, validation and test data by time, so the model is always tested on a period it has never seen.

Include costs. Trading fees, spreads and slippage turn many strategies that look profitable on paper into losing ones.

Define risk limits before any strategy runs: maximum position size, maximum loss per day and conditions that stop the system automatically.

## Level 7-8: Mastery and Strategy

Robust analysis combines several independent signals and focuses on risk rather than prediction: how much can be lost, how quickly and under what conditions.

Markets can be manipulated through practices such as wash trading and coordinated promotion. Data that looks like strong demand may be artificial, which is another reason to verify sources.

## Common Mistakes

Trusting a backtest without out-of-sample testing leads to strategies that fail with real money.

Treating AI summaries or social media sentiment as reliable signals without verification leads to decisions based on noise or false information.

## Hands-On Project

Using only historical data, build a simple analysis that compares one on-chain metric with later price changes. Split the data by time, report the result on the unseen period, include trading costs, and write down why the result may not hold in the future.
