# Blockchain Fundamentals For Business

## Overview

Blockchain Fundamentals for Business explains how blockchains work, what they are genuinely useful for in commerce, and where a normal database is the better choice. It is education, not investment advice.

## Level 1-2: Foundations

A blockchain is a shared ledger where records are grouped into blocks, and each block is linked to the previous one by a cryptographic hash. Changing an old record would change its hash and break every link after it, which makes tampering evident.

A public blockchain, such as Bitcoin or Ethereum, lets anyone read it and take part. A permissioned blockchain limits participation to approved members, such as companies in a supply chain.

A wallet holds the private keys that control assets on a blockchain. Whoever holds the private key controls the funds, and a lost key usually means the funds are lost permanently.

## Level 3-4: How It Works

Consensus is the method a network uses to agree on the next block. Proof of work uses computing effort to secure the chain, while proof of stake uses participants' locked-up tokens as security.

A smart contract is a program stored on a blockchain that runs automatically when its conditions are met, such as releasing a payment when a delivery is confirmed.

A stablecoin is a token designed to hold a steady value, usually one U.S. dollar, backed by reserves such as cash and short-term government securities. Stablecoins are the form of crypto most used for business payments.

## Level 5-6: Implementation

Ask whether a blockchain is needed. It helps when several parties who do not fully trust each other need a shared record that none of them controls alone. When one company controls the data, a normal database is cheaper, faster and easier to correct.

Business uses include cross-border payments, supply-chain traceability, tokenized assets and verifiable records such as certificates.

Plan custody carefully. Businesses can hold keys themselves or use a regulated custodian, trading control against the risk of losing keys.

## Level 7-8: Mastery and Strategy

Tokenization represents real assets, such as funds, invoices or property interests, as tokens that can settle faster and be divided more finely. Regulation of tokenized assets is still developing and varies by country.

Strategic value comes from interoperability and trust, not from the technology label. Projects succeed when they remove a real cost, such as slow settlement or reconciliation between companies.

## Common Mistakes

Using a blockchain where a database would do adds cost and complexity without benefit.

Poor key management causes permanent loss. Treat private keys like the keys to a bank vault.

## Hands-On Project

Pick one process in your business that involves several outside parties. Write down who needs to trust whom, whether a shared ledger would remove a real cost, and compare it with a simple shared database on cost and complexity.
