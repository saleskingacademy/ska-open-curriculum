# Crypto Compliance And Risk

## Overview

Crypto Compliance and Risk teaches the legal and operational risks a business faces when it handles digital assets, and the controls used to manage them. It is educational and is not legal advice; laws differ by country and change often.

## Level 1-2: Foundations

Know-your-customer rules require certain businesses to verify the identity of their customers. Anti-money-laundering rules require monitoring for suspicious activity and reporting it to authorities.

In the United States, businesses that transmit value on behalf of others may be money services businesses under FinCEN rules, which brings registration and anti-money-laundering obligations. Many states also require money transmitter licences.

Sanctions rules prohibit dealing with sanctioned people, entities and some addresses. OFAC in the United States publishes sanctions lists that include certain crypto addresses.

## Level 3-4: How It Works

Securities law may apply to tokens. In the United States, courts use the Howey test to decide whether an arrangement is an investment contract: an investment of money in a common enterprise with an expectation of profit from the efforts of others.

Tax reporting applies to digital assets. The IRS treats them as property, and brokers are subject to expanding information-reporting rules for digital asset sales.

Blockchain analytics tools trace the history of funds on public blockchains, which helps screen incoming payments for links to fraud or sanctioned addresses.

## Level 5-6: Implementation

Write a risk assessment covering customers, geographies, products and transaction types, then match controls to the highest risks.

Screen counterparties and addresses against sanctions lists before accepting or sending funds, and keep records of the checks.

Secure custody with multi-signature wallets, hardware security, separation of duties and tested recovery procedures.

## Level 7-8: Mastery and Strategy

Compliance becomes a competitive advantage. Banks, payment partners and enterprise customers work with businesses that can show strong controls, audit trails and clear policies.

Monitor regulatory change. Frameworks such as the European Union's MiCA regulation and new U.S. stablecoin rules change what licences are needed and how reserves must be held.

## Common Mistakes

Assuming crypto is unregulated leads to serious legal exposure. Many existing financial laws already apply.

Weak key custody is an operational risk as large as any legal one. Most large losses come from stolen or lost keys.

## Hands-On Project

Draft a one-page crypto risk register for a business that accepts stablecoin payments: list each risk, its likelihood and impact, the control in place and who owns it. Identify which items need review by qualified counsel.
