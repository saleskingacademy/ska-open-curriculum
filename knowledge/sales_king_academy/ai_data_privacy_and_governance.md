# AI Data Privacy And Governance

## Overview

AI Data Privacy and Governance teaches how to use AI with customer and company data safely and legally, and how to set the policies that keep a team's AI use trustworthy.

## Level 1-2: Foundations

Personal data is any information that identifies a person, such as a name, email address or phone number. Privacy laws such as the GDPR in Europe and state laws such as the California Consumer Privacy Act limit how it may be collected, used and shared.

Data minimization is the principle of sending an AI system only the data a task needs. A call summary does not need the customer's payment details.

Consent and purpose matter. Data collected for one purpose, such as delivering a service, should not be reused for an unrelated purpose without a lawful basis.

## Level 3-4: How It Works

When data is sent to an AI provider, the provider becomes a processor of that data. A data processing agreement sets out what the provider may do with it, where it is stored and how it is deleted.

Redaction removes or masks sensitive fields before text is sent to a model. Automated redaction of emails, phone numbers and account numbers is a common safeguard.

Access control ensures that an AI assistant can only see the data the person using it is allowed to see. An assistant connected to the CRM should respect the same permissions as the user.

## Level 5-6: Implementation

Write an AI use policy: approved tools, data that may never be entered, required human review for customer-facing output, and how to report a problem.

Keep a register of AI systems in use, what data each touches, who owns it and when it was last reviewed.

Log AI-assisted actions that affect customers, such as emails sent or records changed, so any error can be traced and corrected.

## Level 7-8: Mastery and Strategy

Governance becomes a sales advantage. Enterprise buyers increasingly ask how vendors use AI; a clear policy, a processor list and an audit trail shorten security reviews.

Plan for regulation. Rules such as the EU AI Act classify some AI uses as higher risk and require documentation, transparency and human oversight. Building those habits early is cheaper than retrofitting them.

## Common Mistakes

Pasting customer lists or contracts into consumer AI tools can breach contracts and privacy law. Use approved business tools with proper data terms.

Assuming the vendor handles compliance is a mistake. The company that collects the data remains responsible for how it is used.

## Hands-On Project

Draft a one-page AI use policy for your team, list every AI tool currently in use with the data it touches, and identify the single highest-risk use that needs a safeguard this month.
