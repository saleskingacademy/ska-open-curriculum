# Crypto Payments Integration

## Overview

Crypto Payments Integration teaches how a business can accept and send payments in digital assets, especially stablecoins, and what changes in accounting, refunds and risk compared with card payments.

## Level 1-2: Foundations

Accepting crypto usually happens through a payment processor that gives the customer a payment address, confirms the transaction on the blockchain and can convert the funds to ordinary currency for the business.

Volatility is the main risk with assets such as bitcoin, whose price can move sharply between the sale and settlement. Stablecoins reduce that risk by tracking a currency such as the U.S. dollar.

Crypto payments are generally final once confirmed. There are no card-style chargebacks, so refunds must be issued as new payments by the business.

## Level 3-4: How It Works

A payment request specifies the amount, the asset and the network. Sending an asset on the wrong network can make funds unrecoverable, so the checkout must be explicit.

Confirmation time depends on the network. Some settle in seconds, others take minutes, and a business decides how many confirmations it waits for before delivering.

Conversion settings decide whether the processor sells the crypto for cash immediately or holds it, which changes the business's exposure to price movements.

## Level 5-6: Implementation

Accounting treatment matters. In the United States, the IRS treats digital assets as property, so a business that receives crypto generally records income at its fair market value in U.S. dollars when received, and later gains or losses when it is sold.

Write a refund policy that states the asset and value used for refunds, since the price may have changed since purchase.

Integrate payment confirmations with your order and CRM systems through signed webhooks from the processor.

## Level 7-8: Mastery and Strategy

Stablecoin settlement can lower costs and speed up international payments, especially for business-to-business transfers across borders.

Regulation is developing quickly. The United States enacted a federal framework for payment stablecoins in 2025, and the European Union's MiCA regulation governs crypto-asset services. Businesses should confirm current rules with qualified counsel before launching.

Note that platform credits, such as prepaid Beats on Sales King Academy, are not cryptocurrency; they are non-redeemable credits for using a service.

## Common Mistakes

Accepting volatile assets without a conversion policy exposes the business to price swings on every sale.

Unclear network instructions at checkout lead to lost customer funds and support problems.

## Hands-On Project

Design the checkout and refund policy for accepting one stablecoin: the network, confirmation rule, conversion setting, accounting entry and customer instructions.
