# AI ROI And Change Management

## Overview

AI ROI and Change Management teaches how to measure the financial return of AI projects and how to lead people through the change so the tools are actually adopted.

## Level 1-2: Foundations

Return on investment compares the gain from a project with its cost. For AI, gains include time saved, revenue added and errors avoided; costs include software, integration work, training and ongoing review.

Adoption decides whether ROI is real. A tool that is bought but not used returns nothing, so change management is part of the investment, not an extra.

A baseline is the measurement of performance before a change. Without it, no improvement can be proven.

## Level 3-4: How It Works

Time saved is converted to value by multiplying hours saved by the cost of those hours, but only counts if the time is redirected to valuable work such as more customer conversations.

Revenue impact is measured with comparisons, such as teams using AI against teams not yet using it, or the same team before and after.

Change management frameworks describe how people move from awareness to adoption: understanding why the change matters, learning the new way, practising it and having it reinforced by leaders.

## Level 5-6: Implementation

Write a one-page business case for each AI project: the problem, the metric, the baseline, the expected improvement, the full cost and the review date.

Train by workflow, not by feature. Show people how their own daily tasks change, with examples from their own work.

Appoint champions in each team who use the tools first, help colleagues and report problems.

## Level 7-8: Mastery and Strategy

At the portfolio level, leaders review AI projects like investments: continue, expand or stop based on measured results.

Culture sustains the gains. When teams are rewarded for improving processes rather than for protecting old ones, AI adoption spreads without constant pushing.

## Common Mistakes

Counting time saved that is never used productively overstates ROI.

Announcing a tool without training or leadership support leads to low adoption and a failed project.

## Hands-On Project

Write the business case for one AI project with a measured baseline, full costs and a ninety-day review date, and a rollout plan that names the champions and the training sessions.
