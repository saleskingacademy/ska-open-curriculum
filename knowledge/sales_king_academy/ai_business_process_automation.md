# AI Business Process Automation

## Overview

AI Business Process Automation teaches how to find, redesign and automate the repeatable processes that run a company, such as intake, approvals, invoicing and reporting, using AI where judgment on text is needed and simple rules everywhere else.

## Level 1-2: Foundations

A business process is a repeatable sequence of steps that turns an input into an output, such as turning a signed contract into an onboarded customer. Automation means software performs some or all of those steps without a person.

Rule-based automation follows fixed instructions, such as moving a file when a form is submitted. AI automation handles steps that need reading or judgment, such as classifying an email, extracting fields from an invoice or drafting a reply.

The best automation candidates are high-volume, repetitive and well-defined. Rare, ambiguous or high-stakes decisions should stay with people.

## Level 3-4: How It Works

Process mapping draws every step, who does it, how long it takes and where handoffs happen. Most delays sit in the handoffs, not the work itself.

Document understanding uses AI to read unstructured documents such as invoices, contracts and forms, and turn them into structured fields that other systems can use.

Exception handling is the design for what happens when automation is unsure. A confidence threshold routes uncertain cases to a person instead of guessing.

## Level 5-6: Implementation

Pick one process and measure its baseline: volume per week, time per item, error rate and cost. Then redesign it before automating, removing steps that add no value.

Build in stages. First the AI suggests and a person approves; then the AI acts on high-confidence cases and routes the rest; finally the process runs with periodic sampling for quality.

Keep an audit trail of every automated action, including the input, the decision and the output, so errors can be found and reversed.

## Level 7-8: Mastery and Strategy

At the strategic level, automation changes how a company scales. A business whose core processes are automated can grow revenue without growing headcount at the same rate, which improves margins and valuation.

Portfolio thinking ranks all candidate processes by value and risk, then funds them in order, so the company gets compounding returns instead of scattered pilots.

## Common Mistakes

Automating before simplifying locks in waste. Remove unnecessary steps first.

Having no exception path causes silent failures. Every automation needs a clear route for cases it cannot handle.

## Hands-On Project

Map one process end to end with times and volumes, redesign it to remove at least one step, and build a pilot where AI handles the text-heavy step with a confidence threshold that routes uncertain cases to a person.
