# Coverage-based completion policy

Effective: 2026-10-03. Applies to the public SKA curriculum, including mathematics, business, sales, engineering and supporting disciplines. This policy supersedes the universal 120,000-word minimum and related fixed chapter/volume minima in older plans and generated catalogue reports. It does not reduce any instructional, assessment, prerequisite, accuracy or originality gate.

## Completion means demonstrated coverage

A subject's scope and competency ceiling determine its necessary length. Foundational subjects may finish well below 120,000 words; broad fields may require several volumes and substantially more. Word count is an inventory metric and a signal for possible gaps. Neither a large count nor a short count decides completion. Add material when it closes an outcome, explanation, practice, application, assessment, remediation or verification gap. Do not add text solely to satisfy a numeric target.

Before COMPLETE, verify a declared scope, prerequisite closure, coherent populated lessons, worked reasoning, meaningful practice, explanatory solutions, quizzes, cumulative/module and final assessments, alternate reassessment, projects or appropriate capstone, scoring rubrics, remediation, taxonomy and computational/AI treatment where relevant. Publish the actual competency ceiling. A complete introductory subject is not the same as complete coverage of its whole research field.

Use the existing state progression: SKELETON, SEQUENCED, INSTRUCTIONALLY POPULATED, ASSESSED, VALIDATED, COMPLETE. A completed lesson block may be committed while its parent course remains SEQUENCED. Planned counts are not populated counts. Legacy source text may be useful without being fully validated. Record unsupported academic-equivalence assertions as review issues rather than repeating them as credentials.

## Preserve source and make progress reproducible

Expand useful material in place or with clearly linked companions. Correct demonstrable errors narrowly; preserve unrelated working explanations. Separate original source instruction from generated study/mapping derivatives. Education-16 and Symbol256 follow academic, taxonomy, prerequisite and assessment validation. Established knowledge, SKA-defined frameworks and experimental hypotheses remain distinct.

Inventories should report word counts with a documented method, populated/planned lessons, actual assessment forms and solution coverage, projects, prerequisite and validation states, and the exact next instructional task. Report percentages separately for declared lesson/outcome coverage; do not invent a single percentage for an undefined programme scope. Older word-target reports remain historical planning artifacts and do not impose current completion requirements.

## Cross-domain dependency rule

Keep mathematics in its declared dependency order. Develop business, sales and engineering entry lessons alongside it only when they require competencies already taught, or teach the missing competency explicitly. Link common mathematics rather than copying whole lessons. Engineering branches require science, measurement, modeling and design evidence; sales requires ethical discovery and verified value claims; business requires operational and financial interpretation, not numerical answers alone.

An educational example is not a live deployment, observed sale, real engineering certification, accreditation or research validation. Professional, legal, safety and changing technology claims require appropriate current authoritative sources before being taught as current requirements. Hypothetical cases must be labeled.
