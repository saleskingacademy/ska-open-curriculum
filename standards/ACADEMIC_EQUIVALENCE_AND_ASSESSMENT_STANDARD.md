# Academic-Equivalent Instruction and Assessment Standard

## Scope and claim boundary
SKA curricula are designed to meet or exceed the instructional depth, sequencing, practice, assessment, and demonstrated competencies normally expected at the stated educational level. "Academic-equivalent" describes curriculum rigor and learning outcomes; it does not mean SKA or a course is institutionally accredited unless an authorized accreditor separately grants that status.

## Required course package
Every complete subject must contain:
1. course description, entry assumptions, prerequisites and dependency graph;
2. measurable program and module learning outcomes;
3. sequenced lessons from zero knowledge through the subject ceiling;
4. textbook-quality study material for every lesson;
5. terminology, notation and reference sheets;
6. worked examples and modeled reasoning;
7. guided practice and independent practice;
8. problem sets or discipline-appropriate exercises;
9. answer keys and explanatory solutions;
10. low-stakes lesson quizzes;
11. module/unit tests;
12. cumulative mid-course assessment where course length warrants it;
13. comprehensive final examination;
14. applied project, laboratory, performance, paper, portfolio, case, implementation or capstone where appropriate;
15. grading rubric and mastery thresholds;
16. remediation and reassessment paths;
17. source/provenance notes for externally verifiable or changing factual material;
18. computational and AI-augmented practice where relevant;
19. accessibility and alternative demonstration considerations;
20. Education-16 placement and Symbol256 metadata after academic/taxonomy validation.

## Assessment blueprint
Assessments must sample the declared outcomes rather than merely repeat lesson wording. A complete course should measure:
- knowledge/recall;
- conceptual comprehension;
- procedural fluency;
- application;
- analysis and error diagnosis;
- multi-step reasoning;
- transfer to unfamiliar situations;
- synthesis/design/modeling;
- evaluation/justification;
- proof, derivation, research, performance or implementation when appropriate.

Each major test requires an assessment blueprint mapping questions/tasks to learning outcomes and difficulty. Correct answers require rationales or scoring criteria. Question banks must include alternate forms to support reassessment without simply repeating the same items.

## Suggested mastery model
Lesson formative quiz: target >=80%.
Module mastery test: target >=80%, with prerequisite-critical skills >=90%.
Cumulative/final assessment: target >=80% overall and no critical competency below 70%.
Capstone/project: must meet every critical rubric dimension.
These are SKA mastery thresholds, not claims about a particular accreditor.

## Level alignment
Foundational: literacy/numeracy and prerequisite competence.
Secondary-equivalent: broad conceptual and procedural command plus independent application.
Advanced-secondary/pre-university: abstraction, multi-step analysis and readiness for introductory tertiary study.
Undergraduate-equivalent: disciplinary foundations, theory, methods, substantial problem solving, projects/labs/writing as appropriate.
Advanced-undergraduate: integration, specialization, independent analysis and complex application.
Graduate-equivalent: advanced theory/methods, critical evaluation, research literacy and open-ended work.
Professional/advanced: standards, real-world constraints, ethics, tool use, complex cases and defensible decisions.
AI-augmented/frontier: effective tool orchestration plus independent verification, limitations, provenance and human accountability.

## Full-test requirement
No subject may be marked complete without cumulative testing. At minimum:
- lesson quizzes throughout;
- a unit/module examination after each major module;
- cumulative checkpoints;
- a comprehensive final;
- a capstone or discipline-appropriate equivalent;
- solutions/rubrics;
- remediation and reassessment.

## Discipline-specific evidence
Mathematics: calculations, derivations, proofs, modeling, computational work.
Science: quantitative problems, experimental design, data analysis, labs/simulations, scientific writing.
English/languages: reading, writing, grammar, listening/speaking where applicable, research, rhetoric, revision and portfolio work.
Computing: code, debugging, algorithms, architecture, security, testing and deployed/project work.
Business: quantitative analysis, cases, strategy, simulations, communication and decisions under constraints.
Humanities/social sciences: primary/secondary source analysis, argument, research, interpretation and evidence evaluation.
Engineering/trades: calculations, design, safety, diagnostics, standards-aware practice and performance tasks.
Health: foundational science, cases, evidence appraisal, ethics and scope-aware reasoning; educational completion never substitutes for required clinical licensure or supervised practice.

## Original-expression policy
Instructional prose, examples, exercises, case studies, explanations, diagrams/specifications, assessment items and SKA curriculum organization should be newly authored. Established facts, equations, methods, terminology and ideas are taught accurately without pretending those underlying facts are proprietary. Third-party expression is not copied into the corpus unless its license and provenance explicitly permit the intended use.

## Completion gate
COMPLETE requires content coverage + outcome coverage + assessment coverage + verified solutions/rubrics + prerequisite closure + taxonomy validation + capstone + final examination. Word count is a descriptive inventory/coverage signal, not a mandatory minimum or completion criterion. See [the coverage-based completion policy](COVERAGE_BASED_COMPLETION_POLICY.md).
